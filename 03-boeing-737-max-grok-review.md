# Adversarial review: Boeing 737 MAX

## Verdict
REVISE. The draft owns one exclusive question — how much operator recovery a design may assume when the operator has not been given the system model — and it keeps the five rival diagnoses visible instead of collapsing into greed, delegation, or foreign-pilot failure. The architecture is the right one: 30 March 2016, three genuine options, a counterfactual that refuses to treat disclosure as a cure, and an honest moderate on whether re-issuing the assessment would have moved the hazard class. It is not publishable as it stands. The choice screen tells the player what the FAA will require in 2020; “in front of you” loads Coordination Sheet Rev. D onto Teal’s table even though the record does not put the sheet in the room and Teal later said he did not know three facts that the ledger treats as facing him; the cold open inverts the morning’s two acts; and the counterfactual’s ET302 bound is built on a sentence that contradicts the chapter’s own previous paragraph and the OIG. The case is the one the book should keep, after a short ordered repair.

## Decision contract audit

**Decision the draft actually teaches.** How much operator recovery may a design assume when the operator has not been given the system model needed to diagnose the failure? The instruments of that question — the un-updated safety case, the FCOM deletion, the four-second runaway assumption — are not a second curriculum. Single-sensor architecture, delegation, airline execution and the training-cost incentive appear as labeled rivals. They do not quietly take the chapter.

**Best decision point.** Approving Rev. D as presented on 30 March 2016: widen the envelope and the authority, carry the single sensor and the repeat logic into ordinary flaps-up flight, leave the formal safety assessment at the high-speed table, and, the same day, ask the AEG to make the function invisible. That package is still reversible a year before the amended type certificate. June 2013 (frame MCAS as an addition to speed trim) and November 2012 (the >10 s simulator point) are earlier and real; they are not cleaner. 2013 set the language; 2012 produced a data point. 30 March changed what the function could do and what the crew would be told, together.

**Nearest overlapping chapter.** Challenger (proof at a go/no-go boundary) and Triangle (a control whose safe failure depends on a person acting in seconds), as the closing line already says. Distinguisher holds: here the person expected to save the machine was not told the machine existed.

## Findings

### BLOCKER

**1. The 2020 return-to-service shape is on the choice screen.**

- *Exact phrase:* option B, “That is how the same function is built on the KC-46 tanker, and it is roughly what the FAA will require in 2020.”
- *Why it fails:* CONTEXT forbids any later-learned fact on the choice screen. A player stopped at “What do you do?” now knows that the authority later required two sensors, one activation and limited authority — the historical verdict arriving as a comparison. The KC-46 comparison was available inside Boeing in 2016 and belongs on the screen. The FAA-RTS list (Safety Items #1–#3, `faa-rts-summary.txt` p. 8 / l. 236–256) was not.
- *Evidence status:* Locator for FAA-RTS holds. The leak is the vehicle, not the tanker.
- *Repair:* Keep KC-46. Drop “what the FAA will require in 2020” from option B. The 2020 fix already sits, correctly, after the reveal (`FAA-RTS` p. 8 and p. 28 / l. 1217–1227).

### HIGH

**2. “In front of you” is not what the record puts in the room.**

- *Exact phrases:* “**Known — in front of you.** Coordination Sheet Rev. D, dated today…”; the 0.2–0.84 Mach / 2.5° numbers; “the single angle-of-attack input and the logic that lets the function fire again five seconds”; the FHA four-second / >10-second sentences; then, under “in the building,” “You will later testify that when you approved the redesign you did not know MCAS ran on one sensor, could fire repeatedly, or that the ten-second result existed.”
- *Why it fails:* The chapter’s own contract, and the dossier, split the day this way: *in the room*, the stall-characteristics problem and a go-forward plan with an “FAA Communication Plan”; *in the building*, Rev. D’s hazard sheet, the 2012 note, the December 2015 AR question, the AEG’s 0.55° understanding, the Southwest clause. House p. 111 / l. 5878–5895 gives the presentation title, the go-forward plan, and Leverkuhn’s approval of the flight-control configuration. It does not put Coordination Sheet Rev. D, or its FHA, on the table in that conference room. What Leverkuhn and Teal were shown beyond the title is an open item in the dossier. Teal’s 11 May 2020 testimony (House p. 28 / l. 1350–1355; p. 123–124 / l. 6625–6638) is a later statement about 2016 knowledge, not a 2016 document. Using it as a marker is the designed move; it is honest only if the three facts he says he did not know are not also labeled as facing him. Single-sensor input and the five-second reset are NTSB-ASR’s description of the *delivered* law (p. 4 / l. 159–166), not established wording on Rev. D. “The record puts all three inside your program before you signed” is the accurate sentence. “In front of you” is not.
- *Evidence status:* Teal’s three unawareness points hold as 2020 testimony. Rev. D’s dated parameters hold as an OIG-T entry for that day (p. 23 / l. 890–909). Presence of the sheet in the room is not established.
- *Repair:* Move Rev. D, the FHA sentences, the carried-over single-sensor / repeat logic, and the Southwest dollar figure into “in the building.” Leave in front of the player what House actually puts in the briefing: the airplane is failing flaps-up stall at low speed; the proposed mitigation is to widen MCAS and give it more authority; there is a June/July FAA simulator and flight plan. Keep giving the *reader* the sheet — that is the case interface — but do not tell them it was on Teal’s table.

**3. The cold open inverts the morning.**

- *Exact phrase:* after the leadership approval, “At 11:16 that morning the program’s chief technical pilot has already sent an email.”
- *Why it fails:* House’s own timeline puts the Leverkuhn/Teal approval at 8:00 a.m. and Forkner’s AEG email at 11:16 a.m. (p. 107 / l. 5667–5674). The executive summary and the MCAS chapter both say the FCOM request came “just hours after” the redesign approval (p. 27 / l. 1283–1284; p. 126 / l. 6744–6745). “Has already sent” makes the deletion precede the expansion. Same day still matters. The order matters, because the AEG is being asked to treat as “WAY outside of the normal operating envelope” a function whose envelope was widened that morning.
- *Evidence status:* 11:16:45 on 30 March is independently attested (House l. 1322, 4311, 6734–6737, 6765). The 3 March date is only footnote 703 (l. 6774) and should stay a receipts note, not a body hedge.
- *Repair:* Approval first, email after. “That morning, at 11:16, after the configuration has been approved…”

**4. The counterfactual’s ET302 bound is false on the name.**

- *Exact phrases:* outcome, “it did not name MCAS”; counterfactual, “Flight 302 had the name in an emergency directive and the procedure in memory.”
- *Why it fails:* The second sentence contradicts the first and the OIG. Emergency AD 2018-23-51 expanded the runaway-stabilizer procedure for erroneous AOA; “neither the bulletin nor the Emergency AD specifically mentioned MCAS” (OIG-T p. 32 / l. 1307–1312; NTSB-ASR p. 11 / l. 556–562). EAIB Finding 51: no MCAS in the FCOM or the AFM. What 302 had was the procedure, not the name. The MOM of 10/11 November did describe MCAS characteristics; the local packet does not establish that this crew had been trained on that MOM as a named system. The bound the chapter wants — telling is necessary and was not sufficient — still stands on the AD’s *procedure*. It does not stand on “the name.”
- *Evidence status:* The AD’s silence on the name is locked by two sources. Whether the MOM reached this crew as a named-system briefing is not locked.
- *Repair:* “302 had the emergency procedure, not the name.” Keep the wheel and the 340-knot mistrim as the physical bound. Do not let the FCOM deletion carry more than the chapter’s own counterfactual will bear (see Finding 8).

**5. “The first officer’s answers were shorter” is not in the record as speech.**

- *Exact phrase:* Lion Air 610, after 23:30:48, “The first officer’s answers were shorter.”
- *Why it fails:* No KNKT or NTSB-ASR line describes the first officer’s spoken answers as shorter. What KNKT records after the handover is that “the activations of manual electric trim up were shorter than the activations of automatic trim down” (p. 80 / l. 2882–2884), and that the first officer’s ANU bursts (1 s, 1 s, 4 s, 2 s, 2 s) did not match the AND runs. “Answers” will be read as talk. That is invented character, or a misreading of trim as dialogue.
- *Evidence status:* Handover time 23:30:48 and recorder stop 23:31:54 hold (KNKT p. 28–29). The speech claim does not.
- *Repair:* “His electric-trim answers were shorter than the nose-down runs,” or drop the clause.

### MEDIUM

**6. Option C’s “as of this morning, that it does not exist” overruns the AEG clock.**

- *Exact phrase:* Reasonably inferable, “the crew who must catch it in four seconds have been told, as of this morning, that it does not exist.”
- *Why it fails:* Forkner asked at 11:16. The FAA “approved it verbally in a subsequent meeting” (OIG-T p. 24 / l. 940–942; draft cold open: “at a later meeting”). The request is same-day. The agreement is not shown to be same-morning. The inference that crews have *already* been told the function does not exist is the outcome of a later verbal act, pulled forward.
- *Evidence status:* Same-day request locked. Same-morning grant not locked.
- *Repair:* “have been asked, this morning, to be told that it does not exist.”

**7. Several load-bearing numbers are blended across sources without a flag.**

- *Exact phrases:* “from about 0.6° to 2.5°—an increase of over 300 percent”; “0.55° high-speed function”; “he answered every one”; “column forces the investigators calculated above 90 pounds” packed with “2.5° of mistrim at 340 knots, forty revolutions.”
- *Why it fails:* OIG-T’s “over 300 percent” is 0.55° → 2.5° (p. 23 / l. 896–897). House/KNKT use 0.6° for the original / high-speed limit (House p. 110 / l. 5826; KNKT p. 192 / l. 7799–7800). Both conversions exceed 300 percent, so the phrase is not false, but the draft attaches the OIG slogan to the House number. NTSB-ASR p. 2 / l. 74–76: the 610 crew “countered each input” for six minutes; “the last few… were not fully countered.” “He answered every one” is too clean. The 90 lb figure is *column* force after autopilot disconnect (EAIB p. 121 / l. 4275–4278, “on average above 90lbs”); the 40 revolutions and 2.5° / 340 kt are wheel/mistrim (EAIB p. 118 / l. 4171–4172; p. 125 / l. 4403, 4427–4431). The sentence is not false. It is mashed.
- *Evidence status:* Each number holds in its home source. The joins are the problem. 0.55 vs 0.6 remains disputed and should stay visibly two figures (AEG understanding vs Coordination Sheet Rev. B), which the inheritance line already does for 0.55°.
- *Repair:* Keep 0.55° for the AEG and 0.6° for the sheet; do not hang “over 300 percent” on 0.6°. “Countered each until the last few.” Separate column force from wheel revolutions.

**8. Judgement overweights the FCOM relative to the chapter’s own counterfactual.**

- *Exact phrase:* “high that keeping the function in the manual and the differences course was the last cheap reversible act, and that its removal was made on a description of the airplane that had stopped being true that morning.”
- *Why it fails:* The counterfactual, once Finding 4 is fixed, says disclosure plus a simulator changes the accidents in degree, not in kind, and that “telling was necessary and was not sufficient.” That is the right bound (JT043 recovered without the name; 302 had the procedure and could not finish the wheel). A “last cheap reversible act” that the chapter itself says would not have changed the kind of the later problem is a process judgement dressed as a causal one. What 30 March uniquely buys is the chance to *ask* whether four seconds, one sensor and repeat still hold at 2.5° and low speed with the vane-failure alerts on — not a guaranteed reclassification, and not a guaranteed save from a page in the FCOM.
- *Evidence status:* Teal’s “the certification plans would have to be updated” holds (House p. 112 / l. 5950–5952). NTSB-ASR p. 5 / l. 250–253: the FHA was re-run in the simulator after the change and the classification did not move. Both are already in the draft. The confidence line forgets them.
- *Repair:* Keep high confidence that the case should have been re-issued and that the AEG was briefed on the old airplane. Keep moderate that the class would have moved. Move FCOM-as-cheap-act down, or recast it as the last cheap way to *test* the recovery assumption, not as the act that would have saved 302.

**9. The inheritors are listed more than they are given decisions.**

- *Exact phrases:* “the engineer in Jakarta who read that log”; the AEG representative and the FAA delegating engineer as a roll-call; “both answered and both dropped.”
- *Why it fails:* AGENT and the Triangle exemplar require state, information and a remaining choice. The 043 crew have a third pair of eyes and a log they under-wrote — that is a decision. The 610 and 302 crews have hours and a sequence; they are not asked what they could still preserve. The Jakarta engineer had a log that named IAS/ALT disagree and FEEL DIFF PRESS and not the shaker or the cutout, and he released the airplane after an ADM flush and a connector clean (KNKT p. 38–39 / l. 1528–1537). That is a decision. The AEG representative said yes on a 0.55° high-speed model (OIG-T p. 24 / l. 945–948). That is a decision. “Dropped” for the 2015 AR and the June 2016 engineer is House’s “ultimately dismissed” (p. 116 / l. 6190), which is an investigator finding about the *program*, not those two people’s last act.
- *Evidence status:* Hours and ages hold and are used fairly: 610’s crew are not novices (6,028 / 5,174 total; FO 4,286 on type, which the draft omits but does not invert). 302’s first officer is 361 hours, MAX-rated the same day as the NG, FO authority from 1 February 2019 (EAIB p. 34 / l. 1326–1348) — “a month into line flying” is about five or six weeks. The afterlife then refuses to convert those hours into the foreign-pilot story. The hours are not the problem. The missing choices are.
- *Repair:* One sentence of remaining choice each for the Jakarta engineer, the AEG, and one accident crew. Do not invent names (KNKT and EAIB give none).

**10. June 2013 and November 2012 are present, not tested, as earlier candidates.**

- *Exact phrases:* role-card constraint, “the regulator has already accepted the ‘addition to speed trim’ framing”; building-ledger, the 2012 simulator note. Reconstruction opens on 30 March and stays there.

- *Repair:* Two sentences in reconstruction. Do not move the forced choice.
