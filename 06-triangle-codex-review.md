# Adversarial review: Triangle Shirtwaist Factory

## Verdict

**REVISE.** The chapter owns a strong, exclusive and transferable question about a protective control that can remove another person's recovery path. It cannot pass because the choice screen announces the future fire and trial, while the reconstruction makes the disputed key-dependency more certain than the defence evidence and acquittal permit. It also quotes Leon Stein's Volume II notes as though they preserve exact testimony, despite the receipt's contrary assurance that notes material is only paraphrased. The options and counterfactual make the preferred answer too cheap and too effective by asserting an unproved fitting cost, unproved substitute theft controls, a trade-wide norm, and survival from an inward-opening door above a smoke-filled stair. These are repairable failures around a viable case, so the chapter should be revised rather than cut.

## Decision contract audit

- **Decision the draft actually teaches:** whether an owner may route routine egress through one supervised door while leaving the other nominal exit dependent on a release mechanism that the escaping workers do not routinely use. The intended formulation—whether a control may fail toward containment rather than escape—is exclusive; housekeeping, warning, drills, building design and regulation appear as competing diagnoses, not separate choice questions. The exclusivity nevertheless weakens when option A treats key-free hardware and a named key-holder as equivalent, and when the boundary later declares a keyed exit adequate for a slow hazard.
- **Best decision point:** one specified ordinary closing in late March 1911, before 25 March, when the standing exit routine could still be changed. Calling the routine the “earliest” decision is unsupported because the record does not date its adoption; it is a standing, repeatedly reversible decision and the last routine opportunity is the defensible point. A late-March point also avoids the present contradiction between “three arrangements ... in 1910” (line 37) and evidence from the January 1911 rag removal.
- **Nearest overlapping chapter:** BP Texas City. Both ask whether an apparently ordinary safeguard transfers danger to occupants; Triangle remains distinct only if it stays on the designed release path rather than becoming a general chapter about housekeeping, warning or code.

## Findings

1. **BLOCKER — The choice screen contains explicit hindsight and has no coherent date.** Draft lines 5, 25, 31, 33 and 37 say “eight months before the fire,” “your own later reckoning,” “the judge who later tries you,” “a cuttings fire on the eighth floor,” “the key ... on the day,” “three or four minutes,” and then date the options to 1910. This fails the horizon rule: the reader is told that a fire and trial are coming, is given the future fire's floor of origin, and receives a January 1911 rag removal that cannot be known at an unspecified 1910 choice; the dossier itself says no witness timed the ninth floor, so “three or four minutes” is not even secure hindsight. The death toll, acquittal and reform afterlife do not otherwise enter before the reveal; the suspected leakage is real but bounded to the fire/trial labels, fire location and post-1910 evidence above. **Evidence status:** five prior fires were known to Harris (pp. 1859–62 / lines 54882–55028); the January 1910 inspection card and January 1911 rag removal existed before the final pre-fire closing (p. 1219 / lines 29646–29687; pp. 247–53 / lines 9957–10276); the $25 figure is Harris's later estimate of goods actually found, not a contemporaneous account or total theft (pp. 1867–68 / lines 55212–55271); no cited source establishes “three or four minutes.” **Concrete repair:** fix the choice at one late-March 1911 closing, remove every future-facing label and event-specific fire location, admit only facts demonstrably known or reasonably available by that closing, describe the amount of undetected theft as unknown, and delete the numerical escape time.

2. **BLOCKER — The central key-dependency is stated as a settled standing design when the record does not settle it.** Lines 15, 73, 75 and 105 say “There is a key in its lock,” “its release dependent on a key,” “the arrangement did not guarantee” the key, and “without owning that door at closing or testing” it. The daily Greene Street search is well supported, but defence witnesses said the key was always tied to the knob; superintendent Samuel Bernstein said he used the Washington Place stair as often as ten times a day and “never used a key”; Wortman said foreladies sometimes left there; and two defence witnesses said the door opened during the fire. The People's witnesses establish that particular people could not open the door, not the physical reason, a standing order to lock it, or the nonexistence of an assigned release/test; the jury acquitted under an instruction requiring lock, knowledge and causation. **Evidence status:** observation—operators ordinarily left at Greene past a watchman (lines 14845–14860, 19919–19931, 26021–26048); defence testimony—key on string and regular supervisory use (lines 32097–32115, 41153–41173, 44456–44464, 50661–50671); People's testimony—failed attempts and, for Bucelli, no key seen (lines 13301–13346); unresolved—the fire-day lock state, why attempts failed, owner knowledge, any assigned release duty, and any load test. The “no superintendent on the ninth” proposition is only Bostwick's summation and is not proof that nobody owned the door. **Concrete repair:** make the observed decision the routine concentration of operators at Greene and the normal winter closure/non-use of Washington Place; identify key-dependent failure as the chapter's tested inference, not an adjudicated fact; attribute each fire-day account by side; and delete “nobody owned/tested it” unless direct evidence is found. If the chapter contract requires proof that the standing design itself depended on a key, the current packet does not supply it.

3. **BLOCKER — Volume II “Library notes” are quoted as verbatim testimony.** Line 65 quotes Fannie Selmanowitz (“rubbing against the walls”; “not even a pin”), and line 67 quotes Kate Alterman (“pulled the handle in and out, all ways”) while reconstructing her sequence in close detail. The line 142 receipt says notes material “is paraphrased, not quoted as verbatim,” which is false of the chapter as written. Calling Alterman's source “a researcher's notes” does not make quotation marks safe: the surviving object is Leon Stein's selection and summary, not the lost trial volume, and the exactness of her repeated wording was itself contested. **Evidence status:** Selmanowitz survives only in the notes at lines 27508–27532; Alterman/Alperman only in the notes at lines 28388–28569. Lillian Weiner's “fell into the elevator” quotation at lines 25993–26003 is in the verbatim surviving transcript and may remain; the suspected notes problem does not apply to it. **Concrete repair:** remove quotation marks and closely mimetic phrasing from all notes-only material, identify it once as Stein's summary of the missing volume, paraphrase only what the summary securely conveys, and correct the receipt to match actual practice.

4. **HIGH — The alternatives are not yet demonstrated as institutionally, technically and temporally genuine.** Option A (line 39) bundles a key-free fitting with a named person and calls either a “small cost”; option B (line 41) invents counts, table inspection and shipping-room controls and converts detected goods into “a few waists a year”; option C (line 43) says the arrangement “matches every shop you know” and “passes inspection.” These formulations make the preferred answer artificially cheap and the status quo artificially universal. The record establishes a real theft-control purpose, an unknown amount of undetected loss, inward-opening doors with restricted outward clearance, a single January 1910 inspection result, and the court's statement that no law compelled employers to route employees through more than one door; it does not establish the price or availability of a key-free fitting, those substitute loss controls, or a garment-trade norm. Defence counsel asserted a common practice in “large stores,” which is advocacy, not factory-trade evidence. **Evidence status:** resolved legal boundary—section 80 required every factory door to remain unlocked/unfastened during working hours (lines 61247–61275), while the court said employees need not be routed through more than one (lines 55329–55349); unresolved—hardware, cost, staffing burden, alternative controls and trade prevalence. **Concrete repair:** separate hardware from a staffed release, source the 1911 availability and cost of any device claimed, state the law in the court's exact narrower terms, do not equate found theft with total theft, and replace invented loss controls or support them from a named contemporary source.

5. **HIGH — The counterfactual assumes away the very egress failures it is supposed to test.** Lines 97–101 change the door to one that “opens from inside without a key,” then declare that “an exit exists” and “Some would have lived.” An unlocked latch is not yet a usable exit when the door opens inward against a crowd, the Washington stair is about two feet nine inches wide, eighth-floor evacuees are already in it, and defence witnesses describe smoke or flame rising from below; those facts make survival an unresolved hypothesis, not a result. The draft is commendably explicit that the door would not save everyone and that elevators, fire escape and warning remain impaired, but it still gives the door more causal certainty than the record allows. **Evidence status:** observed/in testimony—door direction (lines 1567–1569), stair width (lines 2014–2034), eighth-floor crowd loading that floor's inward door (lines 46340–46370), and ninth-floor defence witnesses turning back from the Washington stair (lines 32097–32105, 47044–47115); unknown—whether the ninth-floor crowd loaded its door in the same way, timing, stair tenability for each person and survivor count. Brown's eighth-floor experience proves a mechanism, not the ninth-floor state. **Concrete repair:** list the assumptions: the release works without a key; the inward door can open under the actual ninth-floor load; workers reach and recognize it before smoke; the stair remains passable despite eighth-floor traffic; opening does not accelerate smoke entry; and all other systems remain unchanged. The defensible conclusion is that the counterfactual creates an independently attemptable route and may change the problem in kind at the threshold; it cannot assert that anyone survives.

6. **HIGH — The housekeeping diagnosis turns a removed ton into a fire-day ton.** Line 85 says “More than a ton of cuttings had accumulated since January.” Levy testified that 2,252 pounds were removed on 15 January, mostly from the eighth floor, and that no later rag payment/removal appeared; he did not weigh what was present on 25 March. Bostwick later argued that cuttings from more than 100,000 waists had accumulated for two months and ten days and called the material “like tinder,” but that is the prosecution's summation and supplies no fire-day weight. **Evidence status:** observation—last removal and its amount (lines 9957–10000, 10268–10276); contemporary interpretation—Bostwick's accumulation and “tinder” argument (lines 60149–60161); unknown—quantity present at ignition. **Concrete repair:** say that no rag removal after 15 January was established, state the last load only as context, label the 100,000-waist/tinder account as the prosecutor's argument, and do not assign a tonnage to the fire-day fuel.

7. **HIGH — Several competing-diagnosis claims launder advocacy or speculation into fact.** Line 87 says “Give the ninth two more minutes”; line 89 says “the state approved all of it”; line 91 says the arrangement “matched the trade” and “had never mattered in nine years.” No witness gives the ninth a two-minute warning counterfactual, and the dossier expressly says its available time is unknown. “State approved all of it” derives most directly from Steuer's summation about building and Labor Department approval, while the inspection evidence actually establishes one unlocked finding and contains ambiguity over outward-opening practicability; “matched the trade” likewise rests on counsel's large-store assertion. **Evidence status:** observed—the eighth-to-tenth communication path and lack of a call to the ninth (lines 52769–52899), one inspection card (lines 29646–29687), prior fires (lines 54882–55028); contemporary advocacy—state approval/trade normality (lines 55329–55349, 56743–56774); unresolved—minutes gained and industry prevalence. **Concrete repair:** retain warning, code/building and reasonable-practice diagnoses, but label the first as a non-quantified hypothesis and the latter two as defence arguments bounded by the actual inspection and law.

8. **MEDIUM — “No drill” is asserted after the judge struck that evidence.** Line 63 says “The ninth floor received no telephone warning and no drill,” and the choice screen makes drills part of the surrounding risk. The telephone-warning statement holds; the no-drill statement does not survive the trial record as admissible proof. **Evidence status:** the transcript contains witness references to no drills, but the court expressly struck all such references and instructed the jury to disregard them (p. 1212 / lines 28927–28944). **Concrete repair:** remove “and no drill” from factual narration; if retained as an evidentiary limit, say only that the transcript contains references that were struck and therefore cannot establish the fact.

9. **MEDIUM — The human inheritance is vivid but omits two operational decision-makers and misstates Brown's state.** Lines 61–69 give Bucelli, Nelson, Weiner, Levantini, Greenspan, Brown, Lipschitz and Woll genuine information and choices; this is a major success. But the passenger elevators act grammatically by themselves, their operators receive no state or decision, the eighth-floor superintendent Samuel Bernstein disappears, and line 69 says Brown “was not responsible for the door” even though Bernstein had just told him to get the girls out if possible. Blanck's notes also preserve an elevator operator taking a loaded car down after being told to return, which is precisely the kind of inherited, time-bounded decision the chapter promises. **Evidence status:** Brown's instruction and actions are verbatim at lines 46268–46374; Blanck/elevator sequence is notes-only at lines 28718–28730 and must be paraphrased; operator names and complete trip counts are not established by the cited packet. **Concrete repair:** restore Bernstein's fight/evacuate decision and give the elevator operators their observed load, information and choice without inventing names or exact trip totals; change Brown's status to an employee given an improvised evacuation task.

10. **MEDIUM — The reconstruction calls a selected routine “earliest” and turns behavioral inference into consequence.** Line 73 says the rule “decided what three hundred people would think of first.” The record shows routine routing and a reversible standing practice, but not when it began, what every worker first thought, or that habit rather than fire location and crowd visibility caused each path choice. This does not make the selected decision merely early: concentrating ordinary egress was reversible and plausibly consequential to familiarity and flow, but its behavioral effect is an inference. **Evidence status:** observation—ordinary Greene exit; author inference—habit shaped first response; unknown—adoption date and individual cognition. **Concrete repair:** call it the recurring or last reversible decision, say it plausibly shaped familiarity and crowding, and reserve “earliest” for a sourceable adoption point.

11. **MEDIUM — The afterlife is in the right place, but one half remains unsourced.** Line 111 correctly places the “owners locked the doors” memory and reform legacy after outcome and explicitly denies that either proves the 1910 decision. The newly local `cornell-legacy.html` supports the Factory Investigative Commission and ensuing labor-law reform, so that CHECK is now resolvable. No named local source in the packet supports the precise claim that public memory became “the owners locked the doors,” however, and the section should not use the acquittal to suggest that the arrangement itself was adjudged reasonable; the verdict answered a narrower criminal charge. **Evidence status:** reform afterlife resolved at `sources/06-triangle/cornell-legacy.html`, lines 102–106; memory formula unresolved in the named packet; acquittal/verdict resolved at transcript lines 61290–61360 and 61563–61566. **Concrete repair:** add a local, citable afterlife source for the memory formula or describe it more narrowly as the prosecution theory that later summaries repeat; preserve the section's explicit separation from pre-fire evidence.

12. **MEDIUM — The boundary and field test contradict the exclusive recommendation.** Lines 115 and 124 require release to be fail-safe and independent of a person or key, but line 119 says “a keyed door with a person assigned to it can be adequate,” and the pass condition at line 135 bans every one-person or one-key dependency without limiting the exercise to last exits or high-consequence controls. The reader is therefore taught both that key/person dependency is the decision failure and that it is acceptable if the hazard is slow, a condition that can be known only after the hazard arrives. **Evidence status:** this is author method, not a historical fact; the transcript does show that a key in the lock could coexist with successful opening, which makes precision more important. **Concrete repair:** scope the rule to controls whose failure can remove a last independent recovery path; allow keyed security only when an independent fail-open route remains or the control is not life-safety-critical; make the pass condition test that bounded condition. The transfer, solo action and recording method otherwise work outside factories and remain under the reader's control.

13. **LOW — The draft overstates two minor escape details.** Line 53 says “students ... were pulling people across,” while the sources establish one “college boy” helping Nelson and unidentified people helping on the adjoining roof; line 55 says “Some went over the tables,” while Bucelli alone says she jumped onto a table and the question whether she had to do so was excluded. These do not change the diagnosis, but they weaken trust in a chapter built around exact attribution. **Evidence status:** partial at lines 16018–16026 and notes lines 28744–28760; Bucelli at lines 13332–13342; table obstruction only in Stein's notes at lines 27455–27459. **Concrete repair:** use “people on the adjoining roof” and “Bucelli climbed onto a table and broke a window,” or omit both details.

14. **LOW — The receipt block contains material locator and attribution errors.** “Lofts and tenure: p. 1846 / l. 54393” points to the start of Harris's examination, not the tenure evidence; “population ... and no superintendent ... l. 60165–60175” misses both propositions; and “Crain on motive” misidentifies the court as Crain. The notes disclaimer also conflicts with the chapter's actual quotations. **Evidence status:** correct tenure locator is p. 1847 / lines 54408–54437; correct population/no-superintendent passage is p. 2154 / lines 60177–60189 and remains prosecution summation; the court speaks at lines 55322–55349. **Concrete repair:** correct the locators and speaker, mark summation as advocacy, and make every notes label govern prose as well as receipts.

Suspected problem not supported: the chapter does **not** quietly become four separate lessons about housekeeping, drills, fire escapes and regulation. Those appear as competing causes and constraints; the actual loss of exclusivity comes only from the inconsistent definitions of acceptable release inside the chosen control question.

## What survives

- The theft cold open gives the security control a real purpose and prevents a cardboard-villain treatment; retain it after removing the future-fire label and any invented positioning.
- The exclusive release-dependency question is portable and materially different from the nearest cases.
- Lines 55–59 and 107 correctly present failed-opening witnesses, door-opening defence witnesses, acquittal and unresolved lock/knowledge/causation together; that refusal to let outcome decide the door should remain the chapter's evidentiary center.
- The eighth/tenth warning path versus the unwarned ninth is kept distinct, not blurred. The chapter also counts the Greene stair, passenger elevators, fire escape and smoke-filled Washington stair rather than claiming that one door explains every death.
- The named worker journeys are unusually strong human inheritance: they contain partial information, reversals and choices instead of treating workers as a death toll.
- The five competing diagnoses are the right set. Correctly sourced, they keep the preferred mechanism honest without turning into five separate lessons.
- The counterfactual already refuses a survivor count and leaves other systems unchanged; its structure is sound once “some would have lived” and the hidden door assumptions are removed.
- The afterlife section is structurally disciplined, and the transfer/solo exercise is usable outside factories and within a reader's control after its scope is tightened to last independent recovery paths.

## Required revision gate

1. Fix one late-March 1911 choice point and purge every future fire, trial, timing and post-date fact from the choice screen.
2. Recast every ninth-floor Washington Place sentence by evidence class: routine observation, People's witness assertion, defence witness assertion, author inference or unresolved condition; do not assert an unproved assigned-person/test fact.
3. Remove all verbatim-looking language from Stein's Volume II notes and correct the notes disclaimer.
4. Rebuild the options from demonstrably available 1911 actions, with the exact legal boundary, genuine theft exposure, door geometry, and sourced cost/technical feasibility.
5. Correct the cuttings, no-drill, state-approval, trade-practice, warning-time and escape-detail claims; resolve every CHECK and repair the failed locators.
6. Re-run the counterfactual with explicit inward-door, crowd, stair, smoke and recognition assumptions; change survival from assertion to unresolved hypothesis.
7. Restore Bernstein and the elevator operators as downstream decision-makers, then compress the complete chapter to 2,400–3,200 words without removing the acquittal, competing diagnoses or human journeys.

**Acceptance test:** A reader must be able to choose at one stated pre-fire closing using only then-available evidence, while every door claim is attributed, every option is sourceable, no Stein note is quoted, and the key-independent-door counterfactual claims no survivor it cannot prove.
