# AGENT.md — instructions for the writing model

Read `CONTEXT.md`, the selected dossier, and `TEMPLATE.md` completely before drafting.

## What the chapter is for

The reader should experience a hard choice before receiving the explanation. A chapter
is successful when the reader can use its question on a new case, not when they can
repeat the historical verdict.

## Priority stack

1. Never false; never launder inference into fact.
2. Preserve the information available at the decision point.
3. Make the reader choose between plausible alternatives.
4. Follow the people who inherit the danger as decision-makers, not scenery.
5. Extract one exclusive, transferable decision.
6. Tell a compelling story without using suspense to conceal evidence.

## The player character

Open close to the moment of commitment. Establish who “you” are, but do not invent
thoughts or dialogue. Second person is a case interface, not a claim that the historical
actor literally thought what the narration says.

Good:

> You are the launch contractor’s senior manager. Your engineers will not recommend
> launch below the coldest temperature represented in their flight data.

Bad:

> You know the seals will fail, but the customer is impatient.

The second sentence invents knowledge and motive.

## Choice screen

Before the reveal, provide only:

- objective;
- clock;
- current state;
- known evidence;
- reasonable inferences;
- important unknowns;
- resources and constraints;
- two or three plausible options.

Ask for commitment. Also ask what evidence would reverse the reader’s choice.

## Outcome and reconstruction

Reveal the result without treating it as proof. Then move backward to the earliest
reversible and consequential decision. Distinguish decision quality, execution quality
and outcome quality.

## Counterfactual rule

Restore one missing resource, safeguard, piece of information or option. Do not assume
perfect people. Ask whether the later problem changes in kind or only in degree.

## No cardboard villains

Management pressure, schedule, prestige, cost and doctrine are constraints only when
the record supports them. A person may make a poor choice without becoming stupid or
evil. Fraud cases require special care: deliberate deception can eliminate the genuine
uncertainty that makes a useful decision case.

## Receipts

Every important claim in prose maps to a source entry in the dossier. Use short excerpts
only when the exact language matters. Prefer paraphrase plus page or section. Any claim
not yet secured receives an HTML comment in draft form:

`<!-- CHECK: exact claim and required source -->`

## Pre-ship test

1. Could a competent reader reasonably choose either option before the reveal?
2. Did any sentence smuggle the outcome backward?
3. Is the person below management given a real decision?
4. Is the preferred diagnosis compared with another plausible explanation?
5. Does the boundary include a case where the opposite action is reasonable?
6. Can the exercise be completed and reviewed?
7. Can every factual claim be traced to a free source packet?
