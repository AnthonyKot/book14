# Pilot 02 — BP Texas City refinery

Status: **PRELIMINARY — exceptional source packet; exact player role still to lock**

## Short case dossier

1. **The promise management believed**  
   The isomerization unit could be started using familiar procedures despite malfunctioning
   instrumentation, accumulated maintenance problems and a history of abnormal startups.
   Repeated survival had made abnormal conditions appear manageable.

2. **The earliest reversible decision**  
   Pilot decision point: authorization to start the raffinate splitter on 23 March 2005
   while known level-instrument and alarm problems remained. The earlier decisions not to
   connect relief to a flare and to place occupied trailers nearby enter the reconstruction.

3. **What was known at that moment**  
   A work order acknowledged that the tower level transmitter needed repair and deferred it
   until after startup; the blowdown-drum high-level alarm also had a known problem; startup
   was a recognized hazardous phase; previous startups had produced abnormal pressure and
   level behavior.

4. **What remained legitimately uncertain**  
   Whether this startup would repeat earlier abnormal behavior; the actual tower level when
   instruments became misleading; which individual understood the combined significance of
   the degraded safeguards; whether a vapor release would find an ignition source.

5. **Two plausible alternatives**  
   A. Delay startup until the critical instruments are repaired and tested.  
   B. Start with compensating controls: experienced supervision, independent level checks,
   restricted occupancy and an explicit abort threshold.  
   C. Start under existing practice because prior startups had been completed without a
   catastrophic ignition.

6. **Who inherited the consequences**  
   Board operators and outside personnel, particularly contractors working in or near
   temporary trailers close to the ISOM unit.

7. **What they had to do**  
   Operators diagnosed contradictory indications during startup with incomplete procedures
   and limited supervision. People outside the unit had little warning or meaningful control
   once hydrocarbons vented and formed a ground-level vapor cloud.

8. **How the story was later remembered**  
   Immediate accounts could focus on operator actions. The CSB and Baker Panel widened the
   frame to trailer siting, maintenance, instrumentation, process-safety culture, budget and
   repeated uninvestigated abnormalities. The chapter must not erase execution mistakes; it
   must place them inside the conditions management supplied.

9. **The unique decision question**  
   **When degraded safeguards have become normal, what evidence is required before starting
   anyway?**

10. **Primary sources available**  
    CSB final report, urgent recommendations, interview transcripts, internal BP documents,
    animations and the Baker Panel report. Anchor:
    <https://www.csb.gov/bp-america-texas-city-refinery-explosion/>

## Chapter contract

- **Mode:** Decision-forcing case followed by diagnostic reconstruction.
- **Role:** Operations manager authorizing startup; confirm from source record whether a
  more precisely documented role is available.
- **Objective:** Return the unit to production without loss of containment.
- **Decision point:** Before startup authorization on 23 March.
- **Primary resource:** Production time spent to preserve functioning safeguards and
  optionality.
- **Counterfactual:** Repair and function-test the level transmitter and high-level alarm,
  while leaving the atmospheric blowdown system unchanged. This likely improves diagnosis
  but does not remove the catastrophic release path; it tests whether the chapter is wrongly
  reducing a system failure to one instrument.
- **Competing diagnosis:** The direct cause was poor execution of startup procedure rather
  than the authorization decision; alternatively, unsafe blowdown design and trailer siting
  dominated both.
- **Boundary:** Degraded operation can be justified when safeguards are independent,
  compensating controls are tested, authority is explicit and abort criteria are observable.
- **What transfers:** Count simultaneous degraded controls, not merely individual workarounds.
- **What does not transfer:** Refinery process knowledge and regulatory authority.
- **Solo action:** Before starting degraded, list each missing protection and ask whether the
  proposed workaround fails for the same reason.
- **Exercise:** For the next three “temporary workaround” decisions, record the missing
  safeguard, compensating control, owner and abort signal. Pass if every workaround has an
  observable abort signal; review any without one as a decision failure or uncertain.
- **Nearest overlap:** Challenger. Challenger is about evidence outside an operating
  envelope; Texas City is about accumulated degradation inside a repeatedly normalized one.
