# Pilot 05 — Bay of Pigs

Status: **PRELIMINARY — excellent US record; Cuban-side source packet required**

## Short case dossier

1. **The promise management believed**  
   A Cuban exile force could establish itself, stimulate or connect with internal resistance
   and produce political change while direct United States responsibility remained limited
   or deniable.

2. **The earliest reversible decision**  
   Pilot decision point: 16 April 1961, when President Kennedy postponed/cancelled the air
   strikes planned for the morning of the landing. This is not automatically the “cause”; it
   is the cleanest moment at which a key operational assumption changed while the landing
   remained scheduled.

3. **What was known at that moment**  
   The first air strike had not eliminated Castro’s air capability and had weakened surprise;
   the invasion plan depended on control of the air and resupply; overt US involvement carried
   major political cost; the force was already approaching commitment.

4. **What remained legitimately uncertain**  
   How much Cuban air capability remained; whether the landing could secure an airstrip and
   establish a defensible lodgement; the scale of popular uprising or resistance support;
   how Cuba, the Soviet Union and international audiences would interpret further strikes.

5. **Two plausible alternatives**  
   A. Continue the landing while withholding the strikes to preserve political constraints.  
   B. Restore the planned strikes and accept reduced deniability.  
   C. Abort or redirect the landing because an enabling assumption has been removed.

6. **Who inherited the consequences**  
   Brigade 2506 members landing at the Bay of Pigs, supporting air crews and Cuban civilians
   and forces in the battle area.

7. **What they had to do**  
   The brigade had to land, protect limited beachheads, move supplies across difficult exits,
   defend against air attack and await political or military effects that were outside its
   control.

8. **How the story was later remembered**  
   As Kennedy’s failure of nerve, CIA incompetence, exile betrayal or proof the operation was
   impossible from inception. The evidence supports serious structural weaknesses before the
   final air decision, so the chapter must not imply that one restored strike guarantees
   success.

9. **The unique decision question**  
   **When political leadership removes a prerequisite of an operational plan, should the
   commitment continue?**

10. **Primary sources available**  
    FRUS documents, Taylor/Cuban Study Group material, Kennedy records and declassified CIA
    histories. US anchor:
    <https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1961-63v10-12mSupp/summary-x>

## Chapter contract

- **Mode:** Decision-forcing case.
- **Role:** President Kennedy on 16 April, with explicit separation between presidential
  authority and operational knowledge.
- **Objective:** Remove or destabilize Castro’s government without an overt US invasion or
  unacceptable international cost.
- **Decision point:** Before confirming that the dawn strikes will not occur while the
  landing continues.
- **Primary resource:** Deniability spent to preserve combat capability and optionality.
- **Counterfactual:** Restore the dawn strikes with the same landing site, brigade, Cuban
  response and political environment. The test asks whether the problem changes in kind or
  only improves degree; it cannot assume total air supremacy or an uprising.
- **Competing diagnosis:** The operation was structurally unsound before 16 April because
  assumptions about secrecy, uprising, landing geography and US escalation were incompatible.
- **Boundary:** A plan can continue after losing a prerequisite when a validated fallback
  replaces it, the objective changes explicitly, or withdrawal is more dangerous than the
  degraded plan.
- **What transfers:** Re-open authorization whenever a prerequisite is removed; do not treat
  the original approval as approval of the degraded plan.
- **What does not transfer:** Presidential intelligence, covert-action law and alliance costs.
- **Solo action:** Maintain a one-line list of plan prerequisites; when one turns false, mark
  the plan unapproved until a named owner reauthorizes the new version.
- **Exercise:** In the next three projects, record one prerequisite and its owner. Pass when
  any changed prerequisite triggers an explicit continue/modify/stop decision.
- **Nearest overlap:** Iraq. Bay of Pigs loses a prerequisite immediately before execution;
  Iraq tests optimistic assumptions about the system required after initial success.
