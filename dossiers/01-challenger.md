# Pilot 01 — Challenger

Status: **DRAFT 1 WRITTEN — Commission Chapters IV–VI archived; adversarial review pending**

## Short case dossier

1. **The promise management believed**  
   The solid rocket booster joint could be accepted for flight despite predicted cold
   because the available data did not conclusively establish a launch temperature at
   which failure would occur.

2. **The earliest reversible decision**  
   Pilot decision point: the evening of 27 January 1986, during the Thiokol–NASA
   teleconference, when Thiokol management had to maintain or reverse its initial
   recommendation against launch below 53°F. Earlier design and waiver decisions belong
   in reconstruction, not on the initial choice screen.

3. **What was known at that moment**  
   Predicted launch conditions were colder than previous shuttle launches; O-ring erosion
   and blow-by had occurred on earlier flights; Thiokol engineers presented concern about
   delayed sealing in the cold; the data set was small and did not produce a clean failure
   threshold.

4. **What remained legitimately uncertain**  
   The exact joint temperature at ignition; the quantitative relationship between
   temperature and sealing performance; whether the primary and secondary seals would
   function on this flight; how much of the relevant history reached each final decision-
   maker. Private motives are not assumed.

5. **Two plausible alternatives**  
   A. Maintain “do not launch below 53°F” and accept delay without a fully quantified
   failure probability.  
   B. Recommend launch because the evidence did not establish that the joint was unsafe at
   the forecast temperature, while recording residual concern.  
   C. Suspend the recommendation and demand a higher-level review of the complete anomaly
   history before either answer.

6. **Who inherited the consequences**  
   The seven-person flight crew, launch personnel and the engineers whose component was
   committed after their objection.

7. **What they had to do**  
   The crew had no operational remedy for a field-joint failure after booster ignition.
   Downstream agency was therefore concentrated before launch, making this primarily a
   decision-forcing case rather than a rescue case.

8. **How the story was later remembered**  
   Frequently as a morality play about schedule pressure and engineers being ignored.
   The chapter must preserve the genuine weakness and presentation problems in the data,
   the incomplete communication upward, and the difference between “not proven unsafe”
   and “demonstrated safe.”

9. **The unique decision question**  
   **When evidence is sparse because you have never operated in the proposed conditions,
   who carries the burden of proof?**

10. **Primary sources available**  
    Rogers Commission report, chronology, hearing testimony, Thiokol charts and NASA
    records, freely hosted by NASA. Anchor:
    <https://www.nasa.gov/history/rogersrep/v1ch5.htm>

## Chapter contract

- **Mode:** Decision-forcing case.
- **Role:** Thiokol senior manager participating in the 27 January teleconference.
- **Objective:** Support a safe launch decision while giving NASA a usable recommendation.
- **Decision point:** Before management reverses the initial no-launch recommendation.
- **Primary resource:** Schedule time spent to preserve safety margin and information.
- **Counterfactual:** Hold the no-launch recommendation for 24 hours with the same hardware
  and personnel. Does the problem change in kind? Only if the delay also produces warmer
  conditions or a real review; delay by itself is not a repair.
- **Competing diagnosis:** The decisive failure occurred earlier in joint design, anomaly
  normalization and waiver governance; the teleconference merely exposed it.
- **Boundary:** Sparse evidence cannot justify permanent paralysis. If delay creates no
  information or risk reduction and the residual risk is explicitly owned at the proper
  level, proceeding may be reasonable.
- **What transfers:** Reverse the burden of proof when a proposal moves outside the tested
  envelope.
- **What does not transfer:** Launch authority, engineering specialization and the
  irreversibility of solid-rocket ignition.
- **Solo action:** Write the sentence “The proposal is outside our evidence because ___”
  before debating probability.
- **Exercise:** In the next three consequential approvals, mark whether uncertainty comes
  from randomness inside experience or from operating outside experience. Pass when all
  three are classified and one escalation trigger is recorded.
- **Nearest overlap:** Boeing 737 MAX. Challenger concerns approval outside observed
  conditions; Boeing concerns embedding a recovery assumption in design and certification.
