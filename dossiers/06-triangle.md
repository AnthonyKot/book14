# Pilot 06 — Triangle Shirtwaist Factory fire

Status: **PRELIMINARY — rich witness record; management decision needs precise framing**

## Short case dossier

1. **The promise management believed**  
   Production, theft control and worker supervision could be maintained in a high-rise
   garment factory without treating every exit and evacuation path as an independent life-
   safety system.

2. **The earliest reversible decision**  
   Candidate pilot point: the routine decision to keep the Washington Place exit controlled
   or locked during work, combined with the failure to ensure an independently usable exit.
   The trial disputed knowledge and exact conditions; the dossier must lock only what sworn
   testimony establishes.

3. **What was known at that moment**  
   The factory contained large quantities of combustible cloth and scraps; fire was a known
   industrial hazard; hundreds of workers occupied upper floors; usable escape depended on
   stairs, elevators, a fire escape and roof access; employees had limited authority over
   those systems.

4. **What remained legitimately uncertain**  
   Who locked or controlled the door and when; whether owners knew its condition that day;
   how quickly this particular fire could spread; whether each alternative exit would remain
   usable; which later safety standard is fair to apply to a 1911 decision.

5. **Two plausible alternatives**  
   A. Keep every exit immediately openable from inside during occupancy.  
   B. Preserve inspection/theft controls but staff the controlled exit continuously and test
   evacuation capacity.  
   C. Maintain prevailing arrangements and rely on discovery, suppression and multiple
   nominal escape routes.

6. **Who inherited the consequences**  
   Roughly 500 workers, many young immigrant women, along with elevator operators,
   firefighters and bystanders involved in escape and rescue.

7. **What they had to do**  
   Workers chose among smoke-filled stairs, elevators, roof access, an inadequate fire escape
   and windows. Elevator operators repeatedly returned into the building. These were not
   equivalent options and must be reconstructed floor by floor from testimony.

8. **How the story was later remembered**  
   As the fire that transformed US labor law and as a simple story of owners locking workers
   inside. The locked-door evidence is central but was litigated; the chapter must preserve
   that dispute and also avoid erasing wider building, inspection and fire-safety failures.

9. **The unique decision question**  
   **May an organization impose a control whose failure removes another person’s last safe
   exit?**

10. **Primary sources available**  
    Verbatim criminal-trial testimony from about 100 witnesses, survivor interviews,
    photographs, relief records and investigation material in Cornell’s archive:
    <https://trianglefire.ilr.cornell.edu/primary/trialRecords/introduction.html>

## Chapter contract

- **Mode:** Diagnostic reconstruction, then a downstream decision-forcing escape map.
- **Role:** Factory manager responsible for access control; exact historically documented
  role pending trial-record review.
- **Objective:** Maintain production and loss control while keeping the occupied floor safe.
- **Decision point:** The last routine opportunity before the fire to guarantee two
  independently usable exits.
- **Primary resource:** Control over movement spent to gain theft prevention and supervision.
- **Counterfactual:** Make the disputed exit immediately openable while leaving the fire,
  building, workforce and other escape systems unchanged. Test how many later options change
  and avoid claiming that one unlocked door prevents every death.
- **Competing diagnosis:** The dominant failures were combustible housekeeping, inadequate
  fire protection, building design and weak regulation rather than access control alone.
- **Boundary:** Access control may be reasonable when release is fail-safe, obvious, tested
  under load and cannot depend on locating a particular person.
- **What transfers:** A security or process control must fail toward escape, not containment.
- **What does not transfer:** 1911 building code, garment production and legal liability.
- **Solo action:** Identify the control in your environment that can become somebody else’s
  blocked exit—physical, procedural or informational—and test release without its owner.
- **Exercise:** During three routines, mark the last independent exit and whether it depends
  on permission or one person. Pass when each dependency has a tested release path.
- **Nearest overlap:** BP Texas City. Triangle concerns a control that removes escape; Texas
  City concerns degraded safeguards and exposed occupancy around a hazardous process.
