# Adversarial review: Iraq 2003

## Verdict
REVISE. The draft owns the locked question — may you abolish an institution before the replacement, and the mechanism to pay the people you dismiss, exists? — and it refuses, in its own voice, to decide the invasion, de-Ba'athification, or whether the order “caused” the insurgency. The cold open and the choice screen are clean of the aftermath that would have made the exercise a memory test: no demonstrations, no Mosul, no stipend, no Order No. 22, no CMATT, no Inquiry findings, no 2007 testimony. It is not publishable as it stands. Three load-bearing sentences after the reveal contradict the chapter’s own evidence rules (the June 2003 force plan sits in the May ledger; the execution paragraph asserts that the stipend “still excluded the colonels” after the outcome paragraph has said the record does not say so; Tab B is treated as a sentence written to stop the recall). The body is about 4,500 words against a 2,400–3,200 target. The case is the one the book should keep, after a short ordered repair.

## Decision contract audit

**Decision the draft actually teaches.** May you abolish an institution before the thing that must perform its function — including the mechanism for paying the people you are dismissing — exists? The instruments of that question are the 19 May memorandum, Tab B, and Section 3(6). The invasion, Order No. 1, troop levels, and “disbanding caused the insurgency” appear as refused questions or labeled rivals. They do not quietly take the chapter. There is some bleed: the competing-diagnoses section is, by necessity, an insurgency-causation seminar. It is labeled as such and does not rewrite the choice.

**Best decision point.** The signature of CPA Order No. 2 on 23 May 2003, in the window opened by the 19 May memorandum. That is still the right seat and the right day. The early-May OSD–CPA plan is real, and RAND’s “made in Washington… before the deployment” remains a live rival, but no document in this corpus records an instruction that removed the Administrator’s discretion. NSPD-24 is classified. The 19 May paper is a first-person proposal seeking concurrence. Until the signature, ranks still existed, the CFLCC counterparties still had a position, and Section 3(6) was still a tab. After it, they did not. Order No. 1 (16 May) is earlier and interlocking through the Senior Party Member definition; the chapter is right not to make it the pivot.

**Nearest overlapping chapter.** Bay of Pigs (a second phase with no owner) and 737 MAX (in the room versus in the building), as the closing line already says. Distinguisher holds: here the decision-maker wrote the risk down himself, four days early, and signed anyway.

**Seat.** The suspected problem — that Bremer had no discretion, so the chapter is pointed at the wrong person — is not supported by the local corpus. What the corpus supports is a policy formed inside a small OSD–CPA circle that Bremer was inside, not downstream of. A reader who wants the seat moved to Washington still has to name who sat there.

## Findings

### HIGH

**1. The 40,000-man / three-division / 2004–2006 plan is in the May ledger and is not shown to have been in hand that week.**

- *Exact phrase:* “Known — in front of you… The plan you and Slocombe put forward for it is about 40,000 men in three light infantry divisions, the first ready in the autumn of 2004, the last in early 2006.”
- *Why it fails:* CONTEXT forbids later-learned fact on the choice screen. The 19 May memorandum and Tab B announce a New Iraqi Corps and say “numbers will be limited, especially for [officers]” (`bremer-memo-19may2003.txt` l. 258–261). They do not contain 40,000, three divisions, autumn 2004, or early 2006. SIGIR presents those figures as the plan “to replace the disbanded forces” (p. 97 / l. 3682–3686) without dating them to 19–23 May. Chilcot dates the public plan to **23 June**, when Slocombe announced a division of 12,000 in one year and three divisions of 40,000 in two (Chilcot p. 53 / l. 3096–3104). Putting the June numbers in “in front of you” tells the player, before commitment, that a two-and-a-half-year security gap has already been accepted. That is a later plan arriving as a known constraint.
- *Evidence status:* The SIGIR locator holds for the words. The horizon does not. The draft’s own CHECK already asks the right question.
- *Repair:* In the ledger, say only what Tab B says: a New Iraqi Corps, numbers limited, colonels ineligible. Move 40,000 / three divisions / the dated build-out to “What happened,” with the 23 June announcement. Do not let Option A’s exposed risk rest on a timeline that was not on the paper.

**2. The execution paragraph states as fact what the outcome paragraph has just marked unknown.**

- *Exact phrases:* outcome, “whether the colonel-and-above presumption still governed [the stipend] the record here does not say”; reconstruction, “the stipend came in about a month and still excluded the colonels.”
- *Why it fails:* Priority 1: never launder inference into fact. On Point II’s stipend excluded “senior Baath Party members, officers in Iraq’s internal security forces, or those accused of human rights abuses” (p. 117–118 / l. 5648–5650). It does not say colonel-and-above. Bremer told the House the pensions “were always paid” (Hearing p. 186 / l. 3765). Section 3(6) as signed barred both termination pay and pension to anyone deemed a Senior Party Member, and deemed every colonel and above to be one (Order2 casi p. 2 / l. 77–84). Instrument and later practice may have diverged. The outcome paragraph is the honest sentence. The execution paragraph overwrites it, and so makes Section 3(6) carry more than the record will bear.
- *Evidence status:* All three locators hold. The join is the falsehood.
- *Repair:* Keep the outcome paragraph. Rewrite execution to: the stipend came in about a month; On Point II’s published exclusions are party, internal security, and accused abusers; whether colonels who were not in those classes were paid is not established here. Leave Bremer’s 2007 “always paid” in tension with the signed text, not resolved by it.

**3. The Tab B reconstitution clause is treated as a purpose-built foreclosure.**

- *Exact phrases:* afterlife, “a recall running in the field that the order’s own annex was written to stop”; judgement, “Course B was in motion in the field and was foreclosed by a sentence in Tab B.”
- *Why it fails:* The dossier marks this as author inference: the record establishes the clause and establishes the reconstitution; it does not establish that one was written because of the other. The afterlife states the inference as fact, and calls Tab B “the order’s own annex” — Tab B is the payment outline; the signed Annex is the list of dissolved entities (Order2 casi p. 4–5 / l. 132–185). The choice-screen question — “If the answer is ‘nothing, because the army no longer exists,’ ask why Tab B needed a sentence forbidding its reconstitution” — is the right vehicle. The later sentences are not.
- *Evidence status:* Tab B l. 254–257 holds (“riot hired” in the OCR; substance is “not hired”). Reconstitution holds (SIGIR p. 97 / l. 3679; OnPoint p. 113 / l. 5427–5445). Causation is not locked.
- *Repair:* Keep the rhetorical question on the choice screen. After the reveal, say: the clause and the reconstitution are both on the record; reading the first as a response to the second is an inference, and is marked as one.

### MEDIUM

**4. “In the building, not in the room” is drawn, then loaded with later testimony the record does not put on the desk.**

- *Exact phrases:* “Webster: ‘We were surprised, shocked… That was a terrible night.’”; “Major General Tim Cross… told the Iraq Inquiry the decision was taken ‘against all advice from Garner and myself.’”; then Option B, “The land-force negotiations and the brigade-level reconstitution are under way and Garner’s payroll is arranged. Let them continue.”
- *Why it fails:* The honesty sentence that follows — objections reached the senior adviser and the organisation; the record does not establish what reached the desk, or when — is the right sentence, and the draft does **not** slide from “in the building” to “you knew.” The suspected problem is only partly supported. What does fail the 737 MAX device is the emotional cargo. ECLIPSE II, Garner’s 300,000-soldier payroll arrangement, the brigade-level reconstitution, and Slocombe’s “No, you are not doing that” are institutional facts in the CPA/ORHA/CFLCC system (OnPoint p. 93 / l. 4416; p. 113 / l. 5407–5445; p. 114–115 / l. 5496–5516; SIGIR p. 97 / l. 3672–3681). Webster’s “terrible night” is a later interview. Cross’s sentence is 2009 Inquiry testimony. Garner’s protest is “the day it was announced” (OnPoint p. 100 / l. 4816) and the draft correctly puts that protest after the reveal. Tab B already gives the player a contemporaneous reason to know reconstitution was a live category. The later quotations do hindsight’s work that the device does not need.
- *Evidence status:* Locators hold. Presence of those quotations on the Administrator’s desk is not established. On Point II p. 100 / l. 4818–4820 (Bremer’s memoir, via the Army historians) claims he briefed CENTCOM and CFLCC in mid-May; that is later testimony in the opposite direction, and the prose does not use it.
- *Repair:* Keep ECLIPSE II, the payroll arrangement, the reconstitution, and Slocombe’s refusal in the ledger, labeled in-the-building. Move “terrible night” and the Cross Inquiry sentence to after the reveal, or mark them “later testimony about contemporaneous dissent.” Do not let Option B imply that Garner’s payroll printout was on the table.

**5. The inheritors are listed more than they are given decisions, and the chapter never says the corpus has no Iraqi-authored primary.**

- *Exact phrases:* “McKiernan and Webster, who walked around in the dark that night”; “The Iraqi policemen in Mosul who fired on a crowd”; “Petraeus’s eighteen wounded.” The dossier contract: “No Iraqi-authored primary source; the chapter states this.” The draft never states it.
- *Why it fails:* CONTEXT’s human rule is not a roll-call. The forty-five lieutenant colonels who refused to serve without rank (SIGIR p. 150 / l. 5874–5878) have a decision; they are the one downstairs act the chapter actually plays. The dismissed officers are given a condition (no pay, no procedure, unguarded caches) and not a choice. McKiernan and Webster are used to colour the night. There is no named Iraqi speaker. SIGIR, in the same Order No. 2 section the receipts already cite, quotes Ali Allawi on the army as “an integral part of the identity of the state of Iraq” and Laith Kubba calling dissolution “not a smart one” (SIGIR p. 97–98 / l. 3691–3707). Those are SIGIR’s interview records, not Iraqi-authored primaries, and they are the Iraqi voices the packet actually contains.
- *Evidence status:* The gap is in the draft, not the packet. The “no Iraqi-authored primary” fact is locked by the dossier and the manifest.
- *Repair:* One sentence in receipts or in the inheritance section: this corpus has no Iraqi-authored primary; Iraqi voices reach it only through SIGIR, Chilcot and the Army historians. Give Allawi or Kubba, labeled as SIGIR’s record, a sentence of their own. Give the lieutenant colonels the decision they already have. Cut “walked around in the dark” if words are needed.

**6. Resolution 1483 is loaded onto the whole 19–23 May window.**

- *Exact phrase:* “as Administrator you promulgate binding orders in occupied Iraq, under Resolution 1483 and the laws of war.”
- *Why it fails:* Chilcot: “Resolution 1483 was adopted on 22 May 2003” (p. 29 / l. 1616). SIGIR p. 94 / l. 3537 agrees. On the 19 May memorandum the Administrator is proposing an order; 1483 does not yet exist. The signed order of 23 May correctly cites it (Order2 casi p. 1 / l. 5–7). Tab A in the declassified packet already has the 1483 clause in the OCR, which may mean the draft anticipated passage or that the scan mixed versions — the local text cannot settle which. The role card should not treat 1483 as the week’s standing authority.
- *Evidence status:* Adoption date locked by Chilcot and SIGIR. Authority on 19 May is occupation law / presidential appointment / laws of war, not 1483.
- *Repair:* “Laws of war, and, from 22 May, Resolution 1483.” Or cite 1483 only on the signature.

**7. The 2007 Hearing is used, marked, to colour the May objective.**

- *Exact phrase:* “show the Kurdish and Shi’a majority — the populations the old army was used against, and the ones cooperating with the occupation”; CHECK points at Hearing p. 186 / l. 3745–3756 (retrospective) plus the contemporaneous memo line.
- *Why it fails:* “Neither Saddam nor his gang is coming back” is on the 19 May paper (Bremer p. 1 / l. 20–22). The 80 per cent / genocide / 1991-uprising framing is 2007 testimony. The political constraint that Kurdish and Shi’a leaders objected to a reconstituted army is already in the role card as “on the CPA’s account” (Slocombe via SIGIR p. 97 / l. 3668–3671) — itself later, and already hedged. The Hearing sentence is extra, and it is the Administrator’s later justification arriving as his May objective.
- *Evidence status:* Both locators hold. The Hearing is sworn retrospective, as the manifest says.
- *Repair:* Keep the memo’s own words as the objective. Keep the CPA-account hedge on the Kurdish and Shi’a position. Leave the 80 per cent / genocide paragraph in “What happened” or “How the story was remembered,” where the 2007 testimony already sits.

### LOW

**8. “The man who wrote both” over-claims authorship of Tab A.**

- *Exact phrase:* cold open, “the man who wrote both has been in the country for a week.”
- *Why it fails:* The memorandum is first-person and he owns the proposal. SIGIR: the order “was drafted by… Walt Slocombe, in consultation with the Office of the Secretary of Defense” (p. 97 / l. 3660–3663). The draft itself says so two screens later. “Wrote both” is a flourish.
- *Evidence status:* Slocombe-as-drafter holds. Bremer-as-proponent holds.
- *Repair:* “the man who is proposing both.”

**9. Receipts: signature locator is wrong; host of the hinge document is not named.**

- *Exact phrases:* receipts, “Signature: l. 115–116”; the receipts note poor OCR but not that the scan is served from Rumsfeld’s personal library.
- *Why it fails:* Order2 casi l. 115 is “discretion” in Section 6(2). The signature is l. 125–126. MANIFEST and the dossier require the host to be described: a declassified US Defense record, not an official archive. The “catches in the craw” caveat in the body CHECK is otherwise adequate: the surrounding memo is coherent pension policy, the OCR errors (“cut of,” “Increased,” “in principal”) are spelling, and the substance matches Tab B and Sections 3(4)–(6).
- *Evidence status:* Line numbers verified against `cpa-order-02-casi.txt`. Host caveat is in MANIFEST, not in the chapter.
- *Repair:* l. 125–126. One receipts sentence on the host. Page-image check before any quotation is set.

**10. Feith’s memoir is slightly upgraded to “Feith’s record.”**

- *Exact phrase:* reconstruction, “Feith’s record of being briefed by Bremer and Slocombe on 9 May about *their* plan.”
- *Why it fails:* The local packet holds Feith only as Chilcot quotes the memoir (p. 34 / l. 1919–1921). The hand-dealt line, “what he would later call,” is the right register. “Record” is a promotion.
- *Evidence status:* The quotation is Chilcot’s. The memoir is not in the archive.
- *Repair:* “Feith’s memoir, as quoted by the Inquiry.”

### Suspected problems that are not supported

- **SIGIR-only narrative.** Not supported. The draft uses On Point II’s “reasonable premise” (p. 115 / l. 5518–5519) in the reconstruction, distinguishes SIGIR’s “ten days” from On Point II’s 23 June and from Bremer’s “a month,” and keeps the two Mosul dates from merging. SIGIR is the more critical official source; it is not the only voice.
- **Numbers silently mixed.** Not supported as a pattern. 400,000 is Bremer’s MOD figure; 500,000 is SIGIR’s all-forces figure; both are attributed. 5,000 on 26 May is On Point II; eighteen wounded is SIGIR; $45 million by March 2004 is On Point II; $173 million, forty-five lieutenant colonels, 75 per cent, five staff are SIGIR and sit after the reveal. The remaining risk is Finding 1 (40,000 / three divisions) and Finding 2 (colonels and the stipend).
- **“Catches in the craw” hosted on a private library, poor OCR.** Substance holds against the surrounding memorandum (recommend paying most pensions; disqualified persons get neither; delay because there are no rosters). The draft’s page-image CHECK is the right caveat for a draft. It is not yet a publication-ready quotation. See Finding 9.
- **Bremer had no discretion / wrong seat.** Not supported. See Decision contract audit.
- **Cold-open / choice-screen leak of demonstrations, Mosul, stipend, Order 22, CMATT, the insurgency, the Inquiry, or the 2007 testimony.** Not supported. Those arrive after “What do you do?” That is the draft’s strongest method work and should not be disturbed.
