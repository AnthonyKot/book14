# Narrowing brief — Pilot 08, Iraq 2003

You are helping select the **decision point** for one chapter of a decision-forcing casebook.
Your job is **not** to write prose. It is to propose and defend **one dated, owned, reversible
decision** that the chapter will place the reader inside.

## What the book is

Each chapter reconstructs one real choice *before the result was known*, asks the reader to
commit to an option, then follows the people who inherited the consequences. Sister chapters
already locked:

- Challenger — who carries the burden of proof outside the tested envelope? (decision: the
  night-before launch recommendation)
- BP Texas City — what evidence is required to start up with degraded safeguards?
- Boeing 737 MAX — how much operator recovery may a design assume? (decision: 30 March 2016,
  one engineer's approval of a control-law change, one signature, one day)
- Titanic — what changes at the edge of detection?
- Bay of Pigs — should a commitment continue after political leadership removes a prerequisite?
- Triangle Shirtwaist — may a control's release depend on a key or a person?
- Scott vs Amundsen — in a staged plan, what lies beneath the terminal stage?

The Iraq chapter must **not** duplicate Bay of Pigs (a president continuing after a prerequisite
is removed). It must not be a general verdict on the war.

## Hard constraints on the decision you propose

1. **Dated.** A specific day (or a specific week), not a period or a drift.
2. **Owned.** One identifiable person, in a named job, whose act — a signature, an approval, an
   order, a recommendation — is the decision. The reader will be placed in that seat.
3. **Reversible on the day.** At least two options genuinely available to that person then, with
   contemporaneous evidence that both were on the table. Not "they should have stopped the war."
4. **Evidence horizon reconstructible.** What that person knew, and could have known that week,
   must be separable from what was learned later, using *official/primary* records.
5. **Bounded, free, archivable corpus.** Name the specific official documents (report + volume +
   section, or document series) a writer would archive locally. The Iraq Inquiry (Chilcot) is
   ~2.6 million words: if you cite it, cite specific sections, not the report.
6. **Someone identifiable inherits the danger** and has agency of their own.
7. **A live dispute survives.** Name at least one point on which official/primary sources still
   disagree in print, which the chapter will keep open rather than settle.
8. The chapter will **explicitly decline** to resolve the war's legality and morality. Do not
   propose a decision that cannot be taught without resolving them.

## The dossier's provisional decision question

"May you authorize the first phase when success depends on a second phase whose owner,
resources and acceptable end state are unresolved?"

You may keep this question, sharpen it, or replace it — but if you replace it, the replacement
must be a question no sister chapter above already teaches.

## Candidates you must adjudicate (add your own if better)

- **A.** US, 20 January 2003 — NSPD-24 assigning post-war Iraq to the Defense Department and
  creating ORHA eight weeks before invasion.
- **B.** US, 23 May 2003 — CPA Order No. 2, dissolving the Iraqi armed forces and security
  ministries (and its predecessor, Order No. 1, 16 May, de-Ba'athification).
- **C.** UK, 17 March 2003 or the earlier commitment of 28 July 2002 — the decision to
  participate.
- **D.** UK, late 2002 / early 2003 — the decision to take responsibility for southern Iraq
  (MND South-East / Basra) with what preparation existed.
- **E.** US, mid-2002 to early 2003 — force-level and deployment-flow decisions (the TPFDD
  interventions, the rejection of higher troop estimates).
- **F.** Anything sharper you can defend under the constraints above.

## Deliverable

Write a Markdown file with these sections. Be concrete; cite documents, not impressions.

1. **Recommendation** — one decision, one date, one role, in two sentences.
2. **Why it beats each rejected candidate** — one paragraph per rejected candidate, naming the
   constraint it fails.
3. **The seat** — who the reader is: job, authority, what they can and cannot order, what their
   clock is that week.
4. **Options genuinely on the table that day** — two or three, each with evidence they were
   actually proposed or considered at the time.
5. **Evidence horizon** — what was known, reasonably inferable, uncertain, and unknowable on the
   day. Flag anything commonly cited about this decision that is in fact later knowledge.
6. **Who inherits it** — named or identifiable people and what they then had to do.
7. **Live disputes to preserve** — where official/primary sources contradict each other.
8. **Corpus** — the specific documents to archive, with URLs where you know them, marked
   official / primary / secondary, and a note on whether each is free to access.
9. **Decision question** — the one-sentence question this chapter teaches, and why it does not
   duplicate a sister chapter.
10. **Risks** — how this decision could still fail the book's rules, and what would kill it.

Keep it under 1,800 words. Accuracy over completeness: if you are unsure whether a document or
date is real, say so explicitly rather than guessing. Do not invent quotations, document
numbers, or page references.
