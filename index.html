Historical Fuck-Ups

The decision was made upstairs. The danger arrived downstairs. Eight famous disasters, each reconstructed as it looked before the ending was known: what was on the desk, what the real choices were, and who inherited the risk once the choice was made. You are not asked to recite the verdict. You are asked to see the shape — an optimistic promise made by one group, operational danger inherited by another — and to notice it next time, at work, before somebody has to be brave.

Two forms. A reader's cut tells the case plainly, lays out the doors, and asks you not to choose but to think about consequences a year and three years out; cases 6 and 8 are written this way so far, and the rest will follow. A full chapter is the audited version behind it — evidence ledger, competing diagnoses, counterfactual, and receipts with page-and-line locators into archived sources — for the reader who wants to argue. Cases 1, 2, 4, 5 are first drafts; 3, 6, 7, 8 have been through four adversarial reviews. See Method.
