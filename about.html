Cases Method

Method

This is not a catalogue of disasters and it is not a collection of clever verdicts. Each case reconstructs one real choice before the result was known, and then follows the people who inherited the risk.

The thesis, and the test of it

Organisations remember the people who rescued a bad situation more vividly than the people who made it likely. Courage is real. It can also become the last stage of risk transfer: an optimistic promise is made by one group, operational danger is inherited by another, and exceptional action later turns the whole thing into a hero story. The cases test that thesis rather than force it — a case may end as "poor decision, competent execution", "reasonable risk, bad outcome", "distributed failure", or "evidence insufficient".

Rules the cases keep

  • The decision horizon is preserved. Nothing learned later enters the part of the case where you are asked to think. What was on the desk is separated from what was elsewhere in the building.
  • Known, inferable, possible, unknown, unknowable are kept apart, and so are observation, contemporary interpretation, later testimony and the author's inference.
  • Every causal claim gets a counterfactual and a rival. One variable is changed and the question asked whether the later problem changes in kind or only in degree; at least one competing diagnosis stays in the chapter.
  • No cardboard villains. Pressure, schedule, prestige and doctrine are constraints only when the record supports them. Nobody's private motive is inferred from the outcome.
  • The human rule. The person who inherits the danger is not a prop. They get an objective, incomplete information, constraints and choices of their own.
  • Receipts. Every important claim maps to an archived primary or official source with a page-and-line locator. The archive is listed, hashed and rights-noted in the source manifest. Free access does not imply permission to republish, so the documents are cited, not reproduced.

Two forms

A full chapter is the audited version: cold open, role card, evidence ledger, decision screen, outcome, the people who inherited it, reconstruction, competing diagnoses, counterfactual, judgement, boundary, transfer, field exercise, receipts. It exists so that a distrustful reader can argue with the case line by line. Each full chapter after its first draft has been through four independent adversarial reviews (three different model families and a fourth), each checking horizon, locators, disputed points and whether the options were genuinely open.

A reader's cut is the same case told plainly for someone who wants to think rather than audit: where the decision-maker stood, why the choice was not obvious, the doors, and an instruction not to choose but to imagine each door a year and three years out. It carries no verdict the full chapter does not carry, and it links to the full chapter for every fact.

What each case ends with

One decision the reader can make without controlling a whole organisation, and one field exercise: an observable moment, one question, a way to record the answer, a pass condition, a review condition. And the refrain: before somebody has to be brave, where is the last reversible decision?

Status

Eight pilot cases. Cases 3, 6, 7 and 8 are canonical after review; cases 1, 2, 4 and 5 are first drafts. The pilots exist to discover which cases deserve the book — roughly four will be kept. Everything, including the reviews, dossiers and the source manifest, is in the repository.
