# Adversarial review: Iraq 2003

## Verdict

**REVISE.** The draft owns a publishable decision—whether to abolish an institution before its replacement and the mechanism for paying the dismissed exist—and the 19–23 May memorandum-to-signature window is a defensible decision gate. The choice screen nevertheless imports later justifications and figures as though they were available that week, including the 11,000-generals claim, the 40,000/three-division schedule, and part of Bremer's 2007 Kurdish-and-Shi'a rationale. The reconstruction then exceeds the record by calling ownership “unambiguous,” calling the signature the “earliest” consequential choice, and converting the signed exclusion of colonels into claims about later practice that the corpus cannot settle. The same overreach infects the counterfactual: the evidence supports a plausible first-month mitigation, not the asserted change “in kind,” and the draft internally contradicts itself about whether the later stipend still excluded colonels. The alternatives, source packet, boundary, and transfer exercise are strong enough to revise rather than cut, but publication requires a clean horizon, explicit ownership uncertainty, repaired human inheritance, exact locators, and removal of roughly 1,400 words.

## Decision contract audit

**Decision the draft actually teaches:** May the Administrator legally dissolve the regular army, MOD, and associated institutions on 23 May when the replacement force, employee rosters, payment machinery, and officer rebuttal procedure do not yet exist? That is substantially the locked question. The draft occasionally drifts into whether the old army should have been recalled, whether Order No. 1 was right, and how much Order No. 2 contributed to the insurgency, but it normally presents those as alternatives or rival diagnoses rather than answering them.

**Best decision point:** the last documented formal gate is the 19 May proposal through the 23 May signature, with the signature as the forced act. “Earliest reversible and consequential decision” is not proved: the policy was forming in early May, and Slocombe's undated pre-issuance instruction—“No, you are not doing that”—may already have stopped field reconstitution. The chapter can safely claim the signature was the last documented legal gate that converted policy into a binding order; it cannot safely claim no earlier consequential commitment existed.

**Nearest overlapping chapter:** Boeing 737 MAX, because both distinguish what was in the decision document, what existed elsewhere in the organisation, and what the signatory is not shown to have received. Bay of Pigs is the secondary overlap through a missing owner for the next phase.

## Findings

1. **BLOCKER — The forced-choice horizon contains later evidence presented as contemporary fact.** Phrases at risk include “a force with, on Slocombe's later figure, eleven thousand general officers,” “the first [division] ready in the autumn of 2004, the last in early 2006,” and the objective that the Kurds and Shi'a “were … cooperating with the occupation.” **Evidence status:** the 11,000 figure is from Slocombe's 2004 PBS interview as quoted by Chilcot (Chilcot p. 33 / l. 1868–1879); the force schedule is SIGIR's later synthesis, which does not date the plan to 19–23 May (SIGIR p. 97 / l. 3681–3688); and the cooperation/80-percent argument appears in Bremer's 2007 testimony (Hearing p. 186 / l. 3745–3756). The looted-barracks and self-dispersal claims are also later Slocombe/Riley accounts, not evidence shown on Bremer's desk. The demonstrations, Mosul incidents, stipend, Order No. 22, CMATT problems, insurgency assessments, and 2007 admission are correctly kept after the choice; the suspected wholesale outcome leak is therefore not supported. **Repair:** remove the 11,000 and 40,000/timeline claims from the ledger; replace later justifications in the role card with the contemporaneous 16 May Iraqi-leaders meeting and Bremer memorandum; visibly label later accounts as later when they are used to define uncertainty rather than the choice.

2. **BLOCKER — Ownership and the decision point are stated more confidently than the corpus permits.** The claims “the record is unambiguous about who chose it and when,” “the earliest reversible and consequential decision … is the signature,” and “a reader who wants to move the seat to Washington must say who sat there” resolve an expressly unresolved dispute by burden-shifting. **Evidence status:** RAND, known here only through Chilcot, places the decision in Washington before Bremer deployed; Feith's later memoir calls it Bremer and Slocombe's plan; the first-person 19 May memorandum proposes the order and requests Rumsfeld's concurrence; NSPD-24 remains classified. The local corpus contains no instruction that removed Bremer's discretion, while the signed order and Section 6(2)'s exception power support a genuine gate at his desk; I therefore do **not** find that the seat is wrong or that Bremer demonstrably had no discretion. **Repair:** call Bremer the documented proposer and signatory with apparent but not fully recoverable discretion, call 23 May the last documented formal gate, and leave origin/ultimate authority explicitly unresolved.

3. **BLOCKER — Instrument, later payment practice, and counterfactual effect are conflated.** The draft first says the stipend's relationship to the colonel presumption “the record here does not say,” then asserts “the stipend … still excluded the colonels”; it says “a month of dismissed soldiers with nothing” and later that all counterparties were “owed nothing,” despite Sections 3(4)–(5), Bremer's disputed “always paid” testimony, and evidence of multiple later measures. **Evidence status:** Section 3(6) unambiguously barred termination and pension payments to colonels and above **as signed**, subject to a rebuttal procedure; the corpus does not establish how the rule was applied after the policy changes. The counterfactual's conclusion that delay changes the first month “in kind” is also too strong: the 26 May demands included recall, pay, and a new government; the later Mosul protest was reported as financial; and the colonels remain excluded under the stipulated same order. **Repair:** distinguish instrument from implementation in every occurrence, delete “still excluded the colonels” and “with nothing,” and downgrade the counterfactual to “plausibly reduces or may prevent some pay-driven confrontations; kind versus degree is unresolved.” State its assumptions: time needed for rosters; whether payment reaches people on dismissal day; speed and success of rebuttals; whether recall continues while waiting; whether Kurdish/Shi'a leaders tolerate delay; whether regime organisations can re-entrench; and whether the reported protests were primarily financial.

4. **HIGH — The required author inference about Tab B is smuggled in as fact.** The decision prompt asks, “why Tab B needed a sentence forbidding its reconstitution,” and the afterlife says “the order's own annex was written to stop” the recall. **Evidence status:** Tab B does bar hiring people “as reconstituted old units”; the record separately shows negotiations and brigade reconstitution. It does not establish that the sentence was drafted in response to those efforts, and Tab B is not the order's annex. **Repair:** label the connection as author inference at its first use, ask whether the clause *may* respond to reconstitution, and replace “annex was written to stop” with the narrower, documented point that the policy would foreclose unit reconstitution if implemented.

5. **HIGH — Option B is real but receives two unsupported advantages and uncertain authority.** “Garner's payroll is arranged,” followed by “a payroll with names on it,” overreads the record. **Evidence status:** On Point II says Garner arranged for the US Government to pay 300,000 soldiers, not that a usable roster or named payroll existed (OnPoint p. 113 / l. 5441–5448). Nor does the record establish that Bremer alone controlled CFLCC's recall activity or the relevant US funding; his safe unilateral act was to defer his order and seek/permit coordination. The option is still genuine because negotiations, a pay arrangement, and brigade-level reconstitution were in motion. Its political and practical costs are presented fairly—looted facilities, a dispersed army, vetting risk, and Kurdish/Shi'a objections—though the latter is a later reported CPA rationale and should remain attributed. **Repair:** change B's gain to “a funded arrangement and counterparties already being contacted,” not names or a working payroll; define the act as deferral plus coordination, not unilateral command of the military chain.

6. **HIGH — The ratification rival is quoted but not given its full analytical weight.** The draft includes Slocombe and Riley, then answers them with “until [signature] the recall could still be used” and counterparties who “held no position from which to negotiate.” **Evidence status:** the ratification case is stronger than this treatment: there was no fielded national army for the order to remove, facilities were damaged or looted, and a recall might have failed regardless. The opposing evidence is also real: officers and relationships survived, commanders were reconstituting some units, ranks and payment rights had administrative value, and the order formally stopped unit recall. **Repair:** state the rival at its strongest—little immediate combat capacity was destroyed on 23 May—and narrow the chapter's reply to the legal, organisational, and payment capacity the order did change. Do not make the signature carry physical capacity it did not remove.

7. **HIGH — “The people who inherited it” is a catalogue of burdens, not a downstream decision case.** The dismissed officers, Iraqi generals, McKiernan and Webster, brigade commanders, Mosul police, Petraeus's soldiers, Eaton's five-person team, and CPA payroll staff are named, but most receive no decision, available information, or competing objective of their own. “The Iraqi policemen … fired” and “Petraeus's eighteen wounded” are especially prop-like. **Evidence status:** the strongest actual agency in the local record is the Iraqi generals volunteering, the 45 officers refusing rank-and-pay terms, and McKiernan/Webster arguing for a cadre; those can become one or two short decision scenes. The corpus contains Iraqi voices through official intermediaries—SIGIR quotes Ali Allawi and Laith Kubba at p. 97–98 / l. 3689–3707, and On Point reports the 26 May spokesman's demands—but no Iraqi-authored primary source. **Repair:** select two inheritors, give each state/information/options/action, and state explicitly that no Iraqi-authored primary record is in the packet; use an Iraqi voice only with its secondary-source chain visible.

8. **HIGH — The chapter is about 1,334 words over the maximum even before necessary repairs.** **Evidence status:** body text before receipts, excluding CHECK comments, is approximately 4,534 words against a 2,400–3,200 target. The excess is not mainly in the decision: it is repeated background, ownership, payment chronology, new-army detail, and afterlife restatement. **Repair:** use the compression map below and protect the cold open, choice, bounded counterfactual, and field exercise.

9. **MEDIUM — The chapter's exclusive refusal is present but too late and too oblique.** “Not the invasion, not de-Ba'athification” and “declines to decide the war” do not tell the reader before the choice that the chapter refuses to decide invasion legality/wisdom, Order No. 1's overall merits, troop levels, or whether the order caused the insurgency. **Evidence status:** Order No. 1 and insurgency testimony occupy substantial space, so the late boundary permits a quiet second lesson even though the judgement ultimately stays bounded. The suspected claim that the chapter actually decides “disbanding caused the insurgency” is not supported; the competing diagnoses and confidence paragraph leave it contested. **Repair:** add one early sentence naming the four exclusions and keep Order No. 1 only where Section 3(6) imports its definition.

10. **MEDIUM — Several source layers need visible attribution, not only receipt-level attribution.** **Evidence status:** “Feith's record” is a memoir quoted by Chilcot; Slocombe's “army disbanded itself” is a PBS interview quoted by Chilcot; RAND is available only through Chilcot; Hughes and much of the Army material come through historian interviews. The draft correctly says “RAND's account, quoted by the Iraq Inquiry” once, but elsewhere a name followed by a colon can read like a document in hand. No Woodward claim is used substantively, which is good. **Repair:** use formulations such as “Feith later wrote, as quoted by the Inquiry,” “Slocombe said in a 2004 PBS interview reproduced by the Inquiry,” and “the Army historians report from their interviews.”

11. **MEDIUM — Two consequential outcome claims outrun their sources.** **Evidence status:** “A two-year gap before a force existed” confuses the time to complete three divisions with the existence of any new force: Order No. 22 created the army in August 2003, and training activity began in 2003; the plan put the first division in autumn 2004 and the third in early 2006. “The stipend [was tied] to their stopping” appears in the confidence statement, but only Bremer's 2007 testimony makes that causal claim, and it conflicts with uncertainty about the measures and recipients. **Repair:** say “a long gap before the planned three-division force was complete” and attribute the stopped-demonstrations claim to Bremer's later testimony rather than using it as settled causation.

12. **MEDIUM — The Bremer quotation caveat and three receipt locators need correction.** **Evidence status:** page-image inspection confirms the substance of “catches in the craw … heavily militarized society,” but the scan itself appears to read “cut of,” while the draft silently prints “cut off”; this is not merely an OCR corruption in the text file. The receipt caveat also omits that the declassified document is hosted by Rumsfeld's personal library rather than an official archive. In Order No. 2, the signature is at CASI p. 3 / l. 125–126, not l. 115–116; the Annex begins p. 4 / l. 132, not l. 106 or l. 122; and the On Point receipt “p. 117 / l. 5635–5652” crosses to p. 118 at l. 5647. **Repair:** paraphrase “cut off” or print “cut [off]”; identify the private host and image verification; correct the locators listed in the locator audit.

13. **LOW — The boundary is coherent but one sentence should remain a judgement, not a recovered fact.** “The regular army and its pension roll do not meet [the active-danger test], on this record” is defensible as the chapter's diagnosis, while the record also contains the army's history of repression and real Kurdish/Shi'a political risk. **Evidence status:** Option C is not a pure hindsight construction: Bremer's memorandum selectively retained Interior, Military Industrialization, and Higher Education for operational reasons, and the signed order allowed exceptions. **Repair:** retain C and the secret-police/militia reversal, but write that the chapter *judges* the dispersed regular army differently on this record; do not imply the line was uncontested in May.

## What survives

- The cold open's juxtaposition of the warning and the absent mechanism is the chapter's strongest evidence and should remain the hinge.
- “In front of you” versus “in the building, not in the room” is honest where the draft explicitly says the record does not establish what reached Bremer's desk; keep the distinction after removing later material from the choice ledger.
- All three choices are genuine after repair. A is the documented course; B was institutionally in motion though not backed by a proved named payroll; C is supported as available by the memorandum's own selective retention of institutions and the order's exception power.
- The signed order is treated more accurately than either popular slogan: it promised termination payments and continuing pensions, yet Section 3(6) excluded colonels and above as a class subject to rebuttal.
- The draft correctly keeps the ten-day modification, 23 June stipend, and “a month” testimony separate, and it does not merge the two possibly distinct Mosul reports.
- The ratification, dissolution, Order No. 1, process-diffusion, and distributed-cause diagnoses are real rivals, not straw men; the ratification rival needs stronger integration, not deletion.
- The boundary is useful: an active secret police or militia can rightly be abolished before a replacement/payroll exists, which makes C coherent and keeps the transfer from becoming a generic anti-shutdown rule.
- The solo-actionable test—put recipient, entitlement, date, mechanism, and failure condition on the same page as the shutdown—is specific, observable, and within the reader's control.
- The afterlife is generally kept as afterlife rather than proof. Preserve the refusal of both “single blunder made the insurgency” and “nothing was disbanded,” while removing the unmarked Tab B inference.

## Required revision gate

1. Purge or relabel every post-May item in the role card, ledger, and decision screen; state the war/Order No. 1/troop-level/insurgency exclusions before the choice.
2. Recast Bremer as documented proposer/signatory at the last formal gate, not the unambiguous originator; leave authority and the earlier consequential commitment unresolved.
3. Separate Section 3(6)'s signed rule from later practice everywhere, repair the stipend contradiction, and downgrade the counterfactual's “kind” claim while listing its assumptions.
4. Mark the Tab B/reconstitution connection and Option C availability as author inferences; remove the named-payroll claim and give ratification its full capacity-versus-status distinction.
5. Replace the inheritor catalogue with two downstream decisions and disclose the absence of an Iraqi-authored primary source.
6. Correct all CHECKs and failed locators, fix the Bremer quotation/caveat, expose memoir/interview chains, and compress the body to no more than 3,200 words.

**Acceptance test:** A new reader can choose on 23 May using only clearly May-available or explicitly retrievable organisational information, then finish the chapter unable to confuse the signed rule with later practice, Bremer's signature with settled ownership, or plausible mitigation with proved causation.
