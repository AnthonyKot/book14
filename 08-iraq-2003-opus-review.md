# Adversarial review: Iraq 2003

## Verdict

**REVISE.** The case is sound and the seat is right: the 19 May memorandum is the best
hinge document in the pilot so far, the chapter owns one decision, and it does not slide
from "in the building" to "you knew" — the suspicion that it does is not supported by the
text. But one fact in the protected "Known — in front of you" band postdates the
signature (the 40,000-man, three-division, autumn-2004 New Iraqi Corps plan), and it is
load-bearing for both Option A's exposed risk and the counterfactual. The chapter also
omits the two Iraqi assessments that sit in SIGIR between two passages it does cite, and
never states — as the dossier requires — that no Iraqi-authored primary source is in the
corpus; the omission is not neutral, because Ali Allawi contests the Kurdish-and-Shi'a
premise on which Option A's gain rests. Add to that a misdescribed Option A (Tab A as
attached on 19 May contains no colonel bar at all) and a body 40 percent over target, and
the draft is one disciplined pass from publishable rather than ready.

## Decision contract audit

**The decision question the draft actually teaches.** May you abolish an institution
before the thing that must perform its function — including the mechanism for paying the
people you are dismissing — exists? The draft holds this exclusively. It does not ask the
reader to re-decide the invasion, Order No. 1, troop levels, or whether the order caused
the insurgency; Order No. 1 is given as inherited fact in the hand dealt and returns only
as diagnosis 5, correctly, through Section 3(6)'s import of "Senior Party Member." The
stated refusal to decide the war is kept — but it is stated only at line 122 and obliquely
at line 134, both **after** the reader has committed. See MEDIUM 11.

**Best decision point.** The signature of 23 May, and the draft is right to lock there.
One qualification the draft should absorb rather than resist: the memorandum offers two
separable instruments — "In the coming days I propose to issue the attached order (Tab A)…
I could also implement the policies in Tab B on payments" (Bremer p. 1 / l. 7–9) — and the
colonel bar lives only in Tab B on 19 May. The signature remains the right lock, because
Tab B is only a policy until the order carries it; but the separability is a fourth
available course and belongs in the decision screen (HIGH 3).

**Nearest overlapping chapter.** 737 MAX, and the overlap is closer than the dossier
allows: same "in the room / in the building" split, same section furniture, same
"Commit. Then name one observation that would change your answer" beat. The distinguisher
is real and stated at line 156 — here the decision-maker wrote the risk down himself — but
it should appear once early, not only in the closing italic. Bay of Pigs (a decision whose
second phase had no owner) is the secondary overlap and is not at risk.

## Findings

### BLOCKER 1 — a post-signature fact sits in "Known — in front of you"

**Draft:** line 35, "The New Iraqi Corps the order announces will start small. The plan you
and Slocombe put forward for it is about 40,000 men in three light infantry divisions, the
first ready in the autumn of 2004, the last in early 2006."

**Why it fails.** This is the one band CONTEXT.md rule 1 protects absolutely. SIGIR
introduces the plan at p. 97 / l. 3681–3689 with "**To replace the disbanded forces**,
Bremer and Slocombe proposed creating a small volunteer Iraqi army" — a placement after
the order, not before it — and gives it no date. The 40,000 / three divisions / two-year
figures are promulgated in **Order No. 22 of 7 August** (SIGIR p. 149 / l. 5840–5848: "a
tenth of its previous size… three divisions of light motorized infantry battalions over
two years… total end strength… a modest 40,000"). Nothing in this corpus puts a size or a
timetable in front of the Administrator on 19–23 May. The order's own Section 5 says only
"in the near future" (Order2 casi p. 3 / l. 100–104), and Tab A says the same (Bremer p. 5
/ l. 194–199).

**Not decorative.** "A two-and-a-half-year gap before a replacement force exists" is one of
the two pillars of Option A's exposed risk in the dossier and returns in the counterfactual
("the two-year gap before a force exists is still two years", line 112). If the reader is
told in the ledger how long the replacement will take, the choice is easier than it was.

**Evidence status.** Draft's own CHECK #14 already asks the right question and answers
against itself.

**Repair.** Delete the sentence from the ledger. Let Section 5's "in the near future" carry
it, and add to the *Unknown* band: "how long a replacement force takes to exist, and how
big it will be — the order says only 'in the near future'." Move the 40,000/three-division
figures to "What happened," where Order No. 22 already sits.

### BLOCKER 2 — no Iraqi voice, no statement of the gap, and a suppressed counter-source

**Draft:** the whole of "The people who inherited it" (lines 77–83), and the absence, at any
point, of the sentence the dossier §10 requires ("No Iraqi-authored primary source; the
chapter states this"). `grep` for "Iraqi-authored", "no Iraqi", "corpus" returns one hit in
the chapter, and it is about NSPD-24.

**Why it fails.** Two separate failures. (i) CONTEXT.md's human rule: the dismissed are
counted (400,000 / 500,000), described from outside ("rendered these men jobless"), and
shot at in Mosul, but never given state, information or a decision of their own. The one
exception is the forty-five lieutenant colonels who refused to serve without rank and pay —
that is a real Iraqi decision, and it is one clause long. (ii) Selective citation: two
Iraqi assessments sit at **SIGIR p. 97–98 / l. 3690–3707**, directly between two passages
the chapter does cite (l. 3682–3689 and l. 3709). Ali Allawi: the police and internal
security were "detested," but the armed forces "generated considerable sympathy and respect
throughout Iraq"; the public saw the army "as an integral part of the identity of the state
of Iraq," and it was difficult, "even for the Shi'a, to accept a wholesale dissolution of
the armed forces and to leave the country bereft of an army." Laith Kubba: "the measure to
dissolve the Iraqi army was not a smart one… Alienating large numbers of people… was not
a smart move."

Allawi's sentence directly contests the premise on which Option A's gain and Option B's
exposed risk both rest. Skipping the paragraph between two cited paragraphs is the kind of
omission this book's evidence rules exist to prevent.

**Evidence status.** Verified in the local text. Note the correct classification: these are
SIGIR's record of its interviews with Iraqi participants, not Iraqi-authored primary
sources — which is exactly the distinction the chapter must draw as it uses them.

**Repair.** Three sentences. (a) Put Allawi and Kubba into the ledger or the constraint,
against the CPA's account of the Kurdish and Shi'a position, labelled as SIGIR's interview
record. (b) In "The people who inherited it," give one of the dismissed a decision — the
forty-five lieutenant colonels are already there and can carry it, expanded from a clause
to a sentence. (c) State once, in the chapter's own voice: no Iraqi-authored primary source
is in this corpus; every Iraqi in this chapter is reported by an American or British
investigator.

### HIGH 3 — "sign as drafted" misdescribes what was drafted

**Draft:** line 53, "**A. Sign as drafted.** … Termination payments and pensions to follow,
once the data exist, except to senior party members and everyone from colonel up."

**Why it risks failing.** I read Tab A whole (Bremer p. 3–5 / l. 99–208). The draft order
attached on 19 May contains **no Section 3(6), no colonel-and-above deeming clause, no
termination-payment clause, and no conscript release**. Its pension provision (l. 174–182)
reads: "Pensions being paid before 16 April 2003 will continue to be paid, including to
war widows, provided that the power is reserved to the CPA and to the future Iraqi
government to revoke or reduce pensions as a penalty…" — with no Senior Party Member
exclusion of any kind. The colonel bar exists on 19 May only in **Tab B**, the separate
payment policy the memorandum offers separately: "I could also implement the policies in
Tab B" (l. 8–9); "Officers COL and above will be rebuttably presumed to be DPs" (p. 6 /
l. 226).

Between 20 and 23 May the bar was written *into the order*, "disabled veterans" was added
to 3(5), conscripts were released by 3(2), a termination payment became 3(4), and the Annex
grew (the Directorate of Military Intelligence, the Revolutionary/Special/National Security
Courts and the National Olympic Committee appear in the signed Annex, l. 132–180, and not
in Tab A's list).

Three consequences. (i) Option A as written is not the 19 May draft; the reader is asked to
"sign as drafted" a document that was still being drafted. (ii) A fourth genuinely
available course is in the hinge document and missing from the screen: **issue Tab A, defer
Tab B** — dissolve without adopting the colonel bar, which is precisely the Option C move
applied to the payment policy rather than the entity list, and which the memorandum's own
"I could also" makes separable. (iii) This is the strongest evidence in the corpus that the
instrument was the Administrator's to shape inside the four-day window — better evidence
against the "no discretion" hypothesis than the first-person voice of the memorandum,
because it shows the text changing after concurrence was sought.

**Repair.** Rename Option A "Sign the package"; add one sentence in the ledger noting what
Tab A does and does not contain; either add the Tab A / Tab B split as a fourth option or
fold it into Option C as its second limb; and use the Tab A → signed order changes in the
Reconstruction where the ownership dispute is argued.

### HIGH 4 — Section 3(6) is read as self-executing; Section 6(2) is never mentioned

**Draft:** line 73, "You added that pensions 'continued all the way through our time there.
They were always paid.' **Section 3(6), as you signed it, says otherwise** for every officer
of colonel rank and above."

**Why it risks failing.** The order reserves to the same signatory the discretion to except.
**Order2 casi p. 3 / l. 114–115: "The Administrator may grant exceptions any limitations in
this Order at his discretion."** Section 3(4) leaves the termination payment "in an amount
to be determined by the Administrator" (l. 62–63), and 3(6)'s bar is expressly rebuttable
"under procedures to be prescribed" (l. 83–85). The chapter is right that the 2007 sentence
must not overwrite the 2003 clause — that is good discipline and should stay. But "says
otherwise" states as a fact about the instrument something the instrument qualifies twice.
The honest form is: the order barred both payments to colonels and above, made the bar
rebuttable through a procedure that did not yet exist, and reserved to the Administrator a
general power of exception; this corpus does not record how that power was used, so it can
neither confirm nor refute the 2007 claim.

**Evidence status.** Verified. Note this cuts *against* the chapter's rhetoric and *for* its
method — it is the fair version, not a softer one.

**Repair.** One clause citing Section 6(2), and replace "says otherwise" with the bounded
statement. Also fix the ledger at line 45, which imports the signed order's "to be
prescribed" into the pre-signature ledger; on 19 May Tab B says only "will not be paid until
they establish that they are not DPs" (l. 226–229).

### HIGH 5 — the load-bearing constraint rests entirely on retrospective CPA testimony

**Draft:** line 27, "the majority's leaders have told the Coalition — on the CPA's account —
that they will not accept a reconstituted version of Saddam's army"; and the same premise
unhedged at line 53 ("the clean break, and the majority's confidence"), line 55 ("the
majority's leaders react"), line 116, line 126.

**Why it risks failing.** Its two sources are both retrospective and both CPA. SIGIR p. 97 /
l. 3668–3671 places it inside a sentence that begins "**Slocombe later said** that the order
merely reflected facts on the ground… Moreover, the Kurdish and Shi'a leaders objected to
any reconstituted version of Saddam's army." Bremer's version is the 2007 testimony
(Hearing p. 186 / l. 3745–3756). There is no contemporaneous record of the Kurdish or Shi'a
leadership position in this corpus. The nearest contemporaneous item runs the *other* way
and the chapter already has it: Chilcot p. 32 / l. 1795–1799, where on 16 May Iraqi leaders
asked for de-Ba'athification to be *widened* to the security services and army — Iraqi
political leaders wanting more, not the same as the population refusing a reconstituted
army. And Allawi (BLOCKER 2) contests it directly.

The hedge "on the CPA's account" appears once and is opaque: a reader cannot tell it means
Slocombe in 2004 and Bremer in 2007. This premise carries Option A's gain and Option B's
principal risk, so an opaque hedge is not enough.

**Repair.** Name the speaker and the date at first use ("on Slocombe's later account, and
Bremer's in 2007…"), carry the attribution into both options, and set Allawi's sentence
beside it. Do not delete the premise — it is genuinely the CPA's stated reason and Option A
needs it.

### HIGH 6 — the chapter's own confidence leans on the one 2007 sentence it elsewhere refuses

**Draft:** line 118, "moderate that signing before the mechanism made the first month worse
than it needed to be, since the record ties the demonstrations to pay **and the stipend to
their stopping**."

**Why it fails.** The only source in this corpus for the demonstrations stopping when
payment was announced is Bremer himself: "As soon as we announced we were going to start
paying those pensions which we did, the demonstrations of the old army officers stopped"
(Hearing p. 186 / l. 3762–3765) — the sentence immediately before "They were always paid,"
which the chapter (rightly) treats as an interested retrospective. On Point II records the
23 June stipend, the 6 July details and the July disbursements (p. 117–118 / l. 5644–5662)
and does **not** say the demonstrations stopped; SIGIR records the "ten days later"
modification (p. 98 / l. 3717–3719) and says nothing about the effect. Using one half of a
witness's sentence as evidence for your diagnosis while discarding the other half as
special pleading is the failure mode this book's rule 3 exists to catch.

**Repair.** Attribute it — "on Bremer's account the demonstrations stopped once payment was
announced; the corpus carries no independent record of that" — or drop that leg and rest
the moderate confidence on the Mosul protest over "the failure to provide the financial
assistance promised by the CPA" (OnPoint p. 117 / l. 5640–5642), which is the historians'
own finding and carries the point without Bremer.

### MEDIUM 7 — the counterfactual prunes the demonstrators' demands

**Draft:** line 112, "The demonstrations of late May were about salaries, pensions and
recall… Pay on the day plausibly removes the salary and pension grievances and does nothing
about the recall."

**Why it risks failing.** OnPoint p. 117 / l. 5635–5638: on 26 May 5,000 officers and
soldiers demonstrated "against the policy **and the presence of Coalition forces**," and the
spokesman demanded "the recall of the old army, the payment of military salaries, and **the
formation of a new government**." Four demands; the counterfactual's list has three, and
pay-on-the-day touches two. The chapter's own line 67 includes "a new government" and then
drops it eight paragraphs later, at exactly the step where the "changes in kind for the
first month" verdict is reached.

**Repair.** Quote the four demands once and say plainly that timely payment addresses two
of them. The "in kind for the first month, degree thereafter" finding may survive — it is
defensible on the Mosul-over-money evidence — but it must be reached over the full list,
not a pruned one. List the assumptions the counterfactual is making: that rosters could be
built at all within months; that Garner's arranged payroll for 300,000 (OnPoint p. 113 /
l. 5443–5445) would have transferred to CPA use; that the Kurdish and Shi'a position (HIGH
5) would have held during the wait.

### MEDIUM 8 — memoir, PBS interview and RAND report used as if the chapter held them

**Draft:** line 97, "Against both stand **three documents**: Feith's record of being briefed…;
the 19 May memorandum…; and the absence in this corpus of any instruction…"; line 21, "you
and Slocombe briefed Under Secretary Feith on what he would later call 'their plan'"; line
103, "Slocombe: 'We didn't disband the army. The army disbanded itself…'"

**Why it fails.** Feith's account is a 2008 memoir, *War and Decision*, known only through
Chilcot's footnote 126 (Chilcot p. 34 / l. 1917–1921 and fn at l. 1961–1962). Calling it a
"document" that stands beside the 19 May memorandum flattens exactly the distinction the
manifest and the dossier both insist on. Slocombe's "the army disbanded itself" is a **PBS
interview of 26 October 2004** (Chilcot p. 33 / l. 1866, "In a 2004 interview"; fn 123 at
l. 1904–1905); the chapter dates it nowhere, and it is doing work in the constraint at line
27 as well as in diagnosis 3. RAND is handled correctly — "RAND's account, quoted by the
Iraq Inquiry" — and is the model the other two should follow.

**Repair.** Date and label each at first use. Replace "three documents" with something that
does not claim documentary status for a memoir; the memorandum alone carries the point, and
dropping Feith from the count strengthens rather than weakens the paragraph.

**Related, and worth a line rather than a fix:** OnPoint p. 100 / l. 4818–4820 records
Bremer's memoir claim that he "briefed the decision to officials at CENTCOM and CFLCC in
mid-May before announcing it publicly," followed immediately by "Still, numerous US military
and civilian leaders in Iraq at the time have written about their surprise." That is the
contested other half of the "what reached your desk" question. It sits in the chapter's
Receipts (line 152) supporting nothing in the body. It belongs beside line 39.

### MEDIUM 9 — Resolution 1483 arrives inside the four-day window

**Draft:** line 26, "as Administrator you promulgate binding orders in occupied Iraq, **under
Resolution 1483** and the laws of war," cited to the signed order's preamble (Order2 casi
p. 1 / l. 5–7).

**Why it risks failing.** 1483 was adopted on **22 May 2003** — Chilcot p. 29 / l. 1616,
"Resolution 1483 was adopted on 22 May 2003"; SIGIR p. 94 / l. 3537, "On May 22, 2003, the
UN Security Council approved Resolution 1483." That is day 3 of a four-day clock, three days
after the memorandum and the same day Powell learned of the order. Sourcing the role card's
authority to the instrument it produced is circular, and it hands the player a legal basis
that did not exist when the proposal was written.

**Repair.** Either state the sequence — the resolution the order cites was adopted on 22 May,
the day before signature — which is a genuinely interesting fact about the window, or cite
the authority to Franks' Freedom Message and the laws of occupation, which the order also
recites (l. 9–10).

### MEDIUM 10 — one place where SIGIR's frame is taken over the Army historians'

**Why it partly holds.** The chapter does cite On Point II's "reasonable premise" (line 91,
OnPoint p. 115 / l. 5521–5522 — the draft's locator l. 5518–5519 is three lines early). But
it omits the historians' finding four paragraphs on: **OnPoint p. 116 / l. 5556–5558** — "The
policies of de-Baathification and the disbanding of the army were largely successful on the
level of national politics. The major Shia and Kurdish groups became strong allies of the
CPA in its campaign to reshape Iraq's political structure. The consequences of both
policies, however, on the emerging security environment were less salutary." That is the
best evidence in the corpus that Option A's political gain was real and not merely claimed,
from the least critical official source, and it is the natural counterweight to the
sequencing pivot.

**Where the suspicion is NOT supported.** Beyond this one omission the chapter carries the
measured sources fairly: Slocombe and Riley get diagnosis 3 in their own words, Sawers gets
diagnosis 6, Feith's "right decision substantively" position is at least gestured at in
diagnosis 4, and the three-way disagreement on how fast pay was restored is reported without
adjudication. Diagnosis 3 is a real rival, not a straw man, and Tebbit's "not the 10,000
officers" is given as the answer to it rather than as its refutation.

**Repair.** Add the "largely successful on the level of national politics" finding to the
ledger's *inferable-for-signing* band or to diagnosis 6, with its locator.

### MEDIUM 11 — the refusal to decide the war arrives after the reader has committed

**Draft:** line 122, "The chapter keeps all of it, and declines to decide the war," and line
134's "what does not transfer." Both are after the decision screen.

**Why it matters.** The dossier requires the chapter to say in its own voice that it is not
deciding the invasion's lawfulness, Order No. 1's rightness, or the war's causation of the
insurgency — and the point of saying it is to stop the reader from importing a verdict into
the choice. Said only at the end, it is a disclaimer rather than a rule of play.

**Repair.** One sentence between the cold open and "The hand you are dealt."

### MEDIUM 12 — the CHECK apparatus is not what AGENT.md defines

### LOW 13 — locator errors

Listed in the Locator audit below. Five are wrong, all in ways a challenging reader would
hit: the Order No. 2 signature block, the Annex (cited twice, differently, both wrong), the
Section 3(1)–(3) range, and two page attributions that cross a page boundary.

### LOW 14 — small overstatements against source

- Line 71: "Three-quarters of the new force, including almost all its officers and NCOs, **had
  served in the old one**." SIGIR p. 150 / l. 5890–5891: "had **prior military experience**."
  Near-identical in practice, not in what the source says.
- Line 71: "$173 million" is set among Order No. 22's provisions. SIGIR p. 149 / l. 5856–5857
  makes it CMATT's budget drawn from the DFI, not a clause of the order.
- Line 31: "a one-time **termination** payment." Tab B (p. 6 / l. 235–237) says a one-time
  "**transition** payment (equivalent to a month's pay at new scales)"; the signed order
  calls it a termination payment. The ledger is pre-signature, so it should use Tab B's word.
- The Bremer quotations are silently corrected from the OCR — "cut of" → "cut off",
  "Increased" → "increased", "in principal" → "in principle". Correcting is right; doing it
  without a note in the Receipts is not.

### LOW 15 — the provenance caveat is incomplete

Line 146 covers OCR quality but not the host. The manifest requires the memorandum to be
described as a declassified US Defense record served from **Donald Rumsfeld's personal
library**, not an official government archive.

**On the substance of the quotable line, which the brief asks about specifically:** I read
Bremer p. 2 / l. 50–73 whole. The "catches in the craw… heavily militarized society"
sentence sits inside a coherent recommendation and is immediately followed by "For this
reason, I recommend that we pay most pensions — to include military retirees who retired
before 16 April." The argument is internally consistent, the paragraph is not an OCR
artefact, and the substance is not in doubt. The caveat as written is adequate on text
quality and inadequate on host; add the host.

### Suspicions checked and NOT supported

- **"In the building, not in the room" doing hindsight's work.** Not supported. Line 39 ends:
  "The record establishes that these objections reached your senior adviser and your
  organisation. It does not establish what reached your desk, or when." That is the correct
  statement of what SIGIR p. 97 / l. 3672, OnPoint p. 114–115 / l. 5496–5516 and Chilcot
  p. 34 / l. 1928–1930 support. The chapter never writes "you knew" and never uses the
  objections in the Judgement as though they had been received. This is the draft's best
  single paragraph.
- **The seat is not wrong.** If a reviewer wanted to argue Bremer had no discretion, the local
  corpus does not support it and does support the opposite: the memorandum is a first-person
  proposal seeking concurrence (l. 7–11, 64–65); it excludes three ministries by his own
  stated reasons (l. 24–31); the instrument changed materially between Tab A and signature
  (HIGH 3); Section 3(4) leaves the payment amount to the Administrator; Section 6(2)
  reserves him a general power of exception. RAND's "made in Washington" is a secondary
  assertion known only as Chilcot quotes it, and NSPD-24 is classified. The chapter's
  position at line 97 — "a reader who wants to move the seat to Washington must say who sat
  there" — is the right one.
- **Option C as hindsight construction.** Not supported. Bremer p. 1 / l. 24–31 verifies the
  memorandum's own selectivity (Interior kept "because it oversees the police, which we are
  using to help restore order," Military Industrialization for its civil functions, Higher
  Education for the universities). The chapter's use of this at line 116 is exactly right and
  is the best argument in the draft.
- **Numbers mixed across sources.** Not supported. I checked every figure the brief lists.
  Bremer's 400,000 MOD employees (l. 33), OnPoint's ~400,000 officers and soldiers of the
  army (l. 5514–5515), SIGIR's 500,000 across all dissolved forces (l. 3638), Garner's
  300,000 salaries (l. 5443), Miller's "300,000 men with guns" (l. 3646–3648), SIGIR's
  300,000 conscripts (l. 3641), Wallace's "five hundred thousand [sic]" (l. 5678), 11,000
  generals (Slocombe 2004, l. 1875–1877), 45 lieutenant colonels and 75 percent (l. 5884–5890),
  $173m (l. 5856), $45m in stipends by March 2004 (l. 5661–5662), 5,000 on 26 May (l. 5636),
  eighteen wounded (l. 3714–3715). Each is attributed to its owner, and line 77 explicitly
  distinguishes the 400,000 from the 500,000. The three datings of pay restoration (line 69)
  are all three given without a pick — this is the chapter's best source discipline and
  should be pointed at in revision, not touched.
- **The two Mosul incidents.** Not merged. Line 67 keeps them separate and CHECK #20 says why.
- **Competing diagnoses as straw men.** Not supported. All six are real, and the ratification
  argument (3) is given Slocombe, Riley and Tebbit's answer — full weight, correctly
  unresolved.
- **Boundary.** Holds, and it does make Option C coherent: the entities named at line 126
  (Mukhabarat, SSO, Fedayeen) are all in the Annex (l. 143–147, 160–164) and are exactly the
  entities Option C dissolves.
