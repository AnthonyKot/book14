# Adversarial review: Triangle Shirtwaist Factory

## Verdict
REVISE. The draft owns one exclusive question — a control whose release depends on a key, a person or a permission — and it keeps the locked-door dispute open instead of converting the famous prosecution story into fact. That architecture is the right one, and the counterfactual is honest that an opening door does not by itself empty the ninth floor. It still fails a non-negotiable horizon rule: the choice screen tells the reader that “the judge who later tries you” will speak, which is the manslaughter trial leaking into 1910. It also quotes Leon Stein’s Volume II Library notes as if they were spoken words, inverts the January cuttings figure, and at about 3,800 words of prose sits well above the 2,400–3,200 target. The case is publishable after a short, ordered repair; it is not publishable as it stands.

## Decision contract audit

**Decision the draft actually teaches.** May a firm impose a theft-control whose release depends on a key, a person or a permission, when failure of that release removes somebody else’s last independent exit? The draft does not quietly become a chapter about housekeeping, drills, fire escapes or the 1912 statutes. Those appear as competing diagnoses and inherited landscape.

**Best decision point.** The standing ninth-floor exit rule, remade any evening before 25 March 1911: one supervised Greene Street search, Washington Place door closed and keyed, nobody on that floor charged with making the second door passable. That is earlier, more reversible and more consequential than Brown’s pull, Lipschitz’s call, or whoever last turned the key.

**Nearest overlapping chapter.** BP Texas City (safeguard around occupied space) and Titanic (inspection passed, accepted practice, bad outcome), as the closing line already says. Distinguisher holds: Triangle’s second door was not a degraded safeguard; it was designed to depend on a key.

## Findings

### BLOCKER

**1. The manslaughter trial enters the choice screen.**

- *Exact phrase:* “the judge who later tries you will say aloud that no law compels a factory to send its people out of more than one door” (Evidence ledger, Known).
- *Why it fails:* CONTEXT forbids any later-learned fact on the choice screen. The reader is told, before commitment, that this arrangement ends in a criminal trial with a named judge. That is the outcome arriving early. The underlying legal point — Section 80 forbids a locked door during working hours; it does not require two exits — was available in 1910 and belongs on the screen. Crain’s December 1911 remark (p. 1870 / l. 55343–55349) does not.
- *Evidence status:* Locator holds. The leak is the vehicle, not the statute.
- *Repair:* State the contemporaneous legal fact without the trial. Drop “later tries you,” “later reckoning” as a trial-tag, and any other after-the-fire speaker from Known.

### HIGH

**2. Volume II Library notes are quoted as speech.**

- *Exact phrases:* Fannie Selmanowitz “rubbing against the walls going down the shaft,” “not even a pin” (people section); Kate Alterman “pulled the handle in and out, all ways” (same section).
- *Why it fails:* MANIFEST and the chapter’s own receipts say Stein’s notes “are paraphrased, not quoted as verbatim.” The dossier says items marked *(notes)* “may not be quoted as verbatim testimony.” Selmanowitz’s lines are unmarked in the body; the reader cannot tell they are Stein. Alterman is correctly prefaced as notes, then still placed in quotation marks as if those were her words. Stein’s Alterman pages are long comparison excerpts of a lost volume, not a certified transcript.
- *Evidence status:* Selmanowitz notes pp. 763–765 / l. 27519–27529; Alterman notes pp. 1154–1167 / l. 28388–28569. Both sit inside the Library-notes block, not in Volumes I/III/IV.
- *Repair:* Paraphrase both. Keep the Alterman preface. Do not put Stein’s wording in quotation marks.

**3. Fire duration is smuggled into Unknowable.**

- *Exact phrase:* “What three hundred people will do with three or four minutes.”
- *Why it fails:* The dossier locks that no witness timed the ninth floor. Donohue’s 4:45 / 4:48 / 4:55 alarms (pp. 81–83 / l. 3148–3252) are street-box times after the fire, not a ninth-floor clock available to Harris. “Three or four minutes” is author inference from the outcome, placed on the choice screen.
- *Evidence status:* Duration is unresolved. Lipschitz’s “another two minutes” on the telephone (p. 1810 / l. 52899) is her hold time after the 10th answered, not a floor-wide budget.
- *Repair:* Unknowable is crowd behaviour under sudden load, not a numbered minute-count.

**4. The January cuttings figure is inverted, and the eighth/ninth floors are blurred on the choice screen.**

- *Exact phrases:* Known: “Cuttings pile under the cutting tables between rag collections; the dealer took away more than a ton in mid-January.” Competing diagnoses: “More than a ton of cuttings had accumulated since January; the fire started in them.”
- *Why it fails:* Levy’s last rag removal was 15 January 1911, about a ton / 2,252 lb, bulk from the eighth (pp. 246–253 / l. 9957–9970, 10100–10103, 10270–10276). That ton was taken *away* in January. What sat under the tables on 25 March is the unknown accumulation *after* 15 January. Levy also testified there were no cutting tables and no cuttings to remove on the ninth (l. 10169–10231). The player is Harris of the ninth loft; the Known line lets the reader think the floor of 300 operators was sitting on a ton of rags.
- *Evidence status:* Locator range holds; the diagnosis sentence does not.
- *Repair:* Known should say the last collection (eighth/tenth cutting floors, 15 January, more than a ton) and that cuttings accumulate between collections. Diagnosis should not claim a ton had piled up since January.

### MEDIUM

**5. The ninth-floor hose-on-its-rack is not what the cited notes say.**

- *Exact phrase:* “the house hose on the ninth-floor landing was found burned on its rack and had never been tested with water.”
- *Why it fails:* Stern never ran water through the hose (pp. 1254–1255 / l. 30552–30604) — that part holds, and it is the building superintendent’s omission, not Harris’s. The burned-on-the-rack picture is Stein’s notes of Whiskeman: p. 877 / l. 27776–27786 ends “That is Washington Place, 8th floor.” Page 875 scorches the ninth-floor handrail; it does not place a hose-on-rack there. The chapter joins two facts and assigns the wreckage to the wrong landing.
- *Evidence status:* Notes are internally messy; the only explicit floor label on the racked hose is the eighth.
- *Repair:* Keep “never tested with water.” Either drop the rack location or mark it as eighth-floor notes.

**6. Theft-control cost is understated enough to tilt the choice.**

- *Exact phrases:* Known: goods found “ten or fifteen dollars, ‘not over $25.’” Option A: “the small cost of a fitting or a person.” Option C: “it costs nothing, it matches every shop you know.” Known: “A hand-bag search at one door is common in the trade.”
- *Why it fails:* This is not the suspected problem of a costless unlock. Option A does name the unwatched second exit. The record still will not carry the rest. Harris could not say how much was stolen, only what was *found* (pp. 1867–1868 / l. 55206–55271). Steuer, at trial, said large *stores* send people out one door (p. 1870 / l. 55329–55341). Levy compared *cuttings bins* across a few other shops (pp. 264–267 / l. 10707–10822), not bag searches. “Common in the trade” and “every shop you know,” stated as Known / option C, are author inference.
- *Evidence status:* $25 as found value holds. Universal trade practice does not.
- *Repair:* Keep found-value as Harris’s own figure. Move trade-practice to inferable or possible, and bound it (one-exit search used here; Steuer later analogised to stores). Let A’s cost be the unwatched exit, not a “small” fitting.

**7. “Students from the university next door” overreaches the cited lines.**

- *Exact phrase:* Blanck “took his two children to the roof, where students from the university next door were pulling people across.”
- *Why it fails:* Nelson: “a college boy came out from the college” and used a pail to get her up (p. 393 / l. 16022–16026). Blanck notes: people on the next roof pulled girls and his children across; he does not identify them as students (pp. 1949–1954 / l. 28744–28760).
- *Evidence status:* Roof rescue holds; “students” as a class does not.
- *Repair:* “A college boy” / “people on the next roof.” This is CHECK 2.

**8. Elevator operators inherit the danger as scenery.**

- *Exact phrase:* “the two passenger elevators, which made a few more trips… until people began sliding down the cables.”
- *Why it fails:* The dossier names “whoever ran them” among the inheritors. The cars keep running, then stop, as weather. Operators’ information and choice — another trip into smoke, or not — never appear. Ninth-floor workers, Brown, Lipschitz and Woll are given decisions; this class is not. Joe Zito is not in the surviving transcript, so he cannot be invented; the gap is the missing decision, not the missing name.
- *Evidence status:* Selmanowitz notes and Levantini-via-Steuer describe packed cars and a cable descent. No operator testimony is in Volumes I/III/IV.
- *Repair:* One sentence from a passenger’s point of view that the cars kept coming after the loft was already in smoke, then did not. Do not name an operator the packet does not hold.

**9. “No drill” is stated after the court struck that evidence.**

- *Exact phrase:* “The ninth floor received no telephone warning and no drill.”
- *Why it fails:* Witnesses who were asked said there were no fire drills (e.g. Gullo p. 365 area / l. 14884; Monick l. 19940). Crain later struck “one or two” such references and told the jury to disregard them (p. 1212 / l. 28929–28942). The warning half of the sentence is solid (Lipschitz). The drill half treats stricken testimony as a locked fact.
- *Evidence status:* Open, and procedurally withdrawn at trial.
- *Repair:* Drop “and no drill,” or mark it as stricken testimony, not as the floor’s known state.

### LOW

**10. “Most of them young immigrant women” is atmosphere, not a packet count.**

- *Exact phrase:* role card, line 11.
- *Why it fails:* Many witnesses spoke Yiddish or went to the Italian consul; Monick was sixteen. The transcript never totals age or nativity. Cornell’s introduction notes language confusion at trial. The clause is conventional memory, not a receipt.
- *Evidence status:* Uncounted.
- *Repair:* “Most of them women, many of them recent immigrants,” or drop the demography. Not worth a new source hunt.

**11. Bostwick’s population locator is short.**

- *Exact receipt:* “Population per floor and no superintendent on the ninth: Bostwick summation p. 2154 / l. 60165–60175.”
- *Why it fails:* Those lines are still the prior-fires paragraph. 200 / 300 / 60 and “no superintendent… at the ninth floor” are l. 60177–60189, still on or just after p. 2154.
- *Evidence status:* Claim holds; pin is off by about a dozen lines.
- *Repair:* Extend the range to l. 60189.

### Suspected problems that are not supported

- **Prosecution narrative treated as fact.** Not found. Outcome, reconstruction, counterfactual and judgement all keep the door litigated. “Roughly a dozen” versus “two” is a count, not a verdict.
- **Unlock presented as costless.** Not found as a structural fail. Option A names the unwatched exit. Finding 6 is a tilt, not a missing cost.
- **Eighth and ninth floors collapsed.** Not generally. Warning, Brown, Blanck, partition and door-use are kept distinct. The live blur is cuttings (Finding 4).
- **Fire escape, elevators and Greene Street undercounted so the door carries impossible causal weight.** Not found. People and counterfactual send Bucelli, Nelson, Levantini, Greenspan, Selmanowitz and Woll through those routes, and the counterfactual refuses to count lives saved.
- **Quiet second curriculum (housekeeping, drills, escapes, regulation).** Not found. Those are diagnoses and landscape.

## What survives

- The cold open: a working search, a $25-scale theft, a discharge, no fire. The rule is shown succeeding at its stated job.
- The exclusive question, held through reconstruction, judgement, boundary and exercise: what the release depends on, not whether a jury found a lock.
- Refusal to let acquittal prove the arrangement reasonable, or deaths prove the door locked.
- The ninth-floor inheritors as choosers (Bucelli refusing the crush, Levantini turning back from an open door, Greenspan crossing a burning loft).
- Brown on the eighth: same keyed hardware, different floor, a man who pulled.
- Lipschitz’s instrument that could not reach the ninth except through the tenth, and the call she actually made.
- Five live diagnoses, including reasonable practice / bad outcome.
- The counterfactual that changes the problem in kind only at the jamb, and will not count the dead.

2. Remove any numbered minute-count from Unknowable.
3. Paraphrase every Volume II note. No quotation marks on Stein, including Alterman and Selmanowitz. Keep Alterman’s “notes of the lost second volume” frame.

6. Close the five CHECKs per the table: 146 → Cornell introduction; students → college boy / people on the next roof; tables → Bucelli plus notes, no stronger; landings → Whiskeman pp. 59–61; Commission → `cornell-legacy.html`.
