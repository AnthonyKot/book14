# Pilot 08 narrowing — Iraq 2003

## 1. Recommendation

Place the reader as **Tony Blair, Prime Minister, on 31 October 2002**, deciding whether the United States may plan on a UK land contribution — **Package 3**, a divisional headquarters and an armoured brigade, then about 28,000 personnel including reserves — on the same basis as the air, maritime and Special Forces package offered in September. The act is **Letter Wechsberg to Watkins, 31 October 2002, ‘Iraq: Military Options’** (Iraq Inquiry, Volume V, Section 6.1, paras 882–893).

A planning offer, not an invasion order. Last day a land package was still optional, with written advice on both sides that fortnight, while post-conflict owner, resources and end state were unresolved.

## 2. Why it beats each rejected candidate

**A. US, 20 January 2003 — NSPD-24 / ORHA.** Bush signed NSPD-24, lodging post-war Iraq in DOD and creating ORHA under Jay Garner (SIGIR, *Hard Lessons*; CRS RL32370). The directive is **not public** (SIGIR fn. 13; CRS). No text, no meeting record like Wechsberg–Watkins: constraints 4–5 fail. Nearer Scott than a choice between named alternatives.

**B. US, 23 May 2003 — CPA Order No. 2** (Order No. 1, 16 May). Bremer signed “Dissolution of Entities,” abolishing the Iraqi armed forces and listed security ministries (CPA/ORD/23 May 2003/02; Inquiry Vol. X, Section 12.1, paras 148–162). Sharp, but **owned** fails as a clean seat: accounts still disagree whether Bremer decided, implemented an OSD draft, or converted a self-demobilised army into law (SIGIR; 12.1 paras 155–162; Bremer’s 22 May letter to Bush). Also later than the earliest reversible UK land commitment.

**C. UK, 17 March 2003 or 28 July 2002.** The 28 July letter (“I will be with you, whatever”) is a political assurance, not a force-package act with two operational options that day (Inquiry Executive Summary). 17 March — Goldsmith’s short published advice, Cabinet, Cook’s resignation, Commons the next day — **cannot be taught without resolving legality** (constraint 8). Also overlaps Bay of Pigs: continue after the second-resolution prerequisite failed.

**D. UK southern AOR / MND South-East.** The Inquiry finds the UK took **four provinces for six years without a formal Ministerial decision** or robust strategic analysis (Vol. VII, Section 8, key findings). That fails **owned**. Boyce’s later direction that the Phase IV area be “centred on Basra” is a planning assumption, not a dated ministerial act.

**E. US force levels / TPFDD.** Rumsfeld’s pressure on CENTCOM and the Shinseki/Wolfowitz February 2003 exchange are a **period**, not a day. No single signature matches 31 October.

**F considered.** **17 January 2003**, when Blair agreed a large southern deployment (Hoon to Blair, 16 January, ‘Iraq: UK Land Contribution’; Manning to Watkins, 17 January), implements the land option after Turkey killed the northern axis. Hoon even listed “further work to develop a satisfactory plan for the aftermath” as a condition. Opposition that day was about announcement timing, not whether a land package should exist.

## 3. The seat

**You are** the Prime Minister. **You may** tell Washington what it may assume for planning, keep Package 3 off the table, or attach conditions (Turkey, timelines, aftermath). **You may not** authorize war, bind Cabinet or Parliament, compel a US Phase IV plan, or obtain Turkish transit. Defence Secretary Geoff Hoon and CDS Adm Boyce want the land offer this week. Foreign Secretary Jack Straw is in the room. Sir David Manning and Jonathan Powell have argued, in writing, that a military planning cycle is bouncing you ahead of the UN track.

**Clock.** A US–UK draft resolution has been on the Security Council table since 23 October (UNSCR 1441 only on 8 November). MOD minutes of 25 and 29 October say the land option dies around 31 October unless offered like Package 2. CENTCOM has earmarked the US 4th Infantry Division for the north. Turkish elections are days away. Cabinet on 24 October was told military options would come “in due time”; this decision is not going to a Cabinet committee.

## 4. Options genuinely on the table that day

1. **Offer Package 3 for planning, same basis as Package 2** (what Blair did). Hoon, 15 October, and Watkins to Manning, 29 October: keep influence, share risk, and — the MOD’s distinctive claim — **reduce vulnerability to a large post-conflict bill**. Boyce at the meeting: this is not a final commitment to a land war (Wechsberg–Watkins).

2. **Refuse the land package; stay at air/maritime/Special Forces.** Manning’s 16 October minute calls several MOD arguments “pretty dubious,” including the aftermath hedge: Afghanistan showed “once you are in, you’re in deep.” Powell’s note on the 29 October letter: timelines are slipping; “we do not have to decide yet.” FCO (Oakden to Straw’s office, 16 October) called the combat-for-less-occupation trade “optimistic.”

3. **Conditional offer.** Bowen to Manning, 29 October: agree in principle only if the US drops the early-January planning date and leads in the north with Turkish consent. Hoon’s 15 October minute already listed conditions (Turkey, involvement in the final plan, stocks) but **not** a named aftermath owner or stop condition.

## 5. Evidence horizon

**Known that week.** Three packages and rough costs (Johnson to Hoon, 11 October: Packages 2 and 3 together “could be between £1.5bn and £2bn”). The US wants UK bases and some capabilities; a UK land division is **not** militarily required (Manning said so then; Boyce later told the Inquiry the same). The northern option depends on Turkey. UN negotiations are unfinished; inspections have not begun. Whitehall already holds fragmented post-Saddam work — FCO scenario and administration papers, DFID humanitarian notes, Treasury economic impact, SPG papers, JIC 10 October (terrorism) and 23 October (Kurds and Shia), Cabinet Office “models and some questions for post-Saddam government” (Vol. VI, Section 6.4). The 11 October MOD minute called US “Day After” thinking “under-developed.”

**Inferable.** A visible land offer is politically harder to walk back than “planning purposes” admits. A UK division used in the north will leave a UK-shaped aftermath task even if combat is short.

**Uncertain.** Whether CENTCOM will actually exclude the UK if you wait (6.1 paras 957–959: CENTCOM papers still assumed a possible UK land role; “it is not clear what specific information” produced the exclusion warning). Whether Turkey will accept UK troops (JIC 30 October: opposition “probably surmountable, but only by US pressure”). US start date. What “influence” a division buys.

**Later knowledge — keep off the choice screen.** UNSCR 1441 (8 November). Collapse of the northern axis and the 17 January southern three-brigade deployment. NSPD-24/ORHA. Invasion, CPA Orders 1–2, the four-province AOR, the six-year occupation. Actual weapons holdings. Chilcot’s later finding that a combat role **created** post-conflict obligations (6.1 para 961) is outcome, not 31 October fact.

## 6. Who inherits it

**Maj Gen Robin Brims**, GOC 1 (UK) Armoured Division, inherits a land role whose southern form is fixed only in January and whose Basra entry is his (Vol. VII, Section 8). **UK soldiers and reservists** inherit combat then stabilization with kit sized for a short campaign (Vol. VI, 6.3). **Basra officials, police and residents**, then the later four-province AOR, inherit security from a force that planned to thin out. **John Sawers**, Blair’s Special Representative from May 2003, inherits CPA decrees — including Order No. 2 — in which the UK “played little or no formal part” (Executive Summary paras 675–676). Agency: Brims on how to take the city; later commanders on whether to recall local security, pay former soldiers, or patrol a vacuum.

## 7. Live disputes to preserve

- **Was the exclusion threat real?** Hoon/Boyce said US planning assumed no UK land role. Chilcot cannot identify the justifying information and notes CENTCOM was still carrying a possible UK contribution (6.1 paras 953–959).
- **Does a larger first phase reduce second-phase exposure?** Hoon/Boyce said yes; Manning, Oakden and Bowen said no. Later occupation is not proof: the northern option died, the force grew and moved south in January, and the four-province AOR had no formal ministerial owner.
- **Choice or bounce?** Powell’s note and Manning’s “military planning cycle” comment remain live. The Inquiry calls the offer a “political call” and records that it was not put to a Cabinet committee (6.1 key findings).
- **Causal weight.** Invasion, later US CPA orders, and southern execution must stay as competing official diagnoses.

## 9. Decision question

**May you enlarge a first-phase force in order to reduce an unowned second-phase burden?**

Sharpens the dossier question; does not replace its family. **Not Bay of Pigs** — no prerequisite has been removed; the UN track is still open. **Not Scott** — Scott asks what your own last stage silently assumes; this asks whether a bigger first stage is a hedge against someone else’s unspecified later stage. **Not Texas City.** The chapter declines the war’s legality and morality: listed unknowns, not the choice.

## 10. Risks

- **Ratchet smuggled backward.** “Planning purposes” is the contemporaneous description. Later irreversibility belongs in reconstruction.
- **Legality leak.** Goldsmith and 17 March stay off the choice screen. If the draft cannot, this seat is dead.
- **Scott overlap** if framed as “phase two unspecified” rather than “does a bigger phase one buy you out.”
- **Mootness.** Northern Package 3 never fought. Show the *land offer* as the act that made the January southern deployment and later AOR possible, without claiming 31 October selected Basra.
- **Missing originals.** If the October letters cannot be archived except as Chilcot paraphrase, the free-packet test fails.
- **Human bill too thin.** Blair is upstairs. If Brims, Sawers and southern civilians are scenery, the chapter fails.
- **What would kill it.** Evidence that Package 3 was already locked before 31 October, or that Blair could not have stayed at Package 2 that day. Chilcot’s “largely discretionary” finding (6.1 key findings) is why the seat lives.
