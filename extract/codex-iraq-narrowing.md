# Iraq 2003: narrowing recommendation

## 1. Recommendation

Recommend **23 May 2003**: place the reader in **L. Paul Bremer III’s** seat as Administrator of the Coalition Provisional Authority (CPA), deciding whether to sign **CPA Order No. 2, Dissolution of Entities**. The playable act is whether to abolish the Iraqi Ministry of Defence and regular armed services immediately, defer while recall-and-vetting work continues, or dissolve only regime security organs pending an executable replacement plan.

## 2. Why it beats each rejected candidate

**A — NSPD-24, 20 January 2003.** It has a date and presidential owner, but fails the **reconstructible evidence horizon** test. In an official Senate hearing, the Defense Department confirmed the date and purpose but said the Secret directive was “unreleasable for the record”; the directive’s actual text, decision paper, and options therefore could not be verified. Later official summaries cannot show what alternatives President George W. Bush had on his approval page.

**B1 — CPA Order No. 1, 16 May 2003.** This is playable, but loses to Order No. 2 on **identifiable inheritance and causal bounding**. Order No. 1 contained individual-review machinery, and its reach was later changed through implementation and delegation; Order No. 2 transferred a discrete, immediate task—build security institutions after cancelling the old ones—to named planners and commanders.

**C — UK participation, 28 July 2002 or 17 March 2003.** The July point was a private prime-ministerial communication, not an operational order; the March point runs through Cabinet, Parliament, military instructions and legal advice rather than one clean signature. It fails **single ownership**, cannot be taught cleanly while declining legality, and risks duplicating Bay of Pigs: continuing a commitment after a political prerequisite disappeared. The relevant bounded record is Iraq Inquiry Volume II, section 3.3, and Volume III, section 3.8.

**D — UK responsibility for the south.** This fails **dated and owned**. Iraq Inquiry Volume VII, section 8, paragraph 687 says the Inquiry found no evidence that Ministers formally decided the geographical extent of the UK area of responsibility before MND(SE) was established on 12 July 2003. That absence is diagnostically important, but not a playable personal decision.

**E — force levels and TPFDD interventions.** As framed, this is a sequence of estimates, deployment changes and decisions shared among the Secretary of Defense, CENTCOM and the President. It fails **one day/one owner**; selecting one intervention would also require proving that the named actor controlled the resulting occupation force rather than merely one deployment increment.

## 3. The seat

Bremer is the CPA Administrator, newly in Baghdad and in the chain of command through Secretary of Defense Donald Rumsfeld. CPA instruments vested the Administrator with broad temporary executive, legislative and judicial authority; Order No. 2 itself says it enters into force on signature. Bremer can sign, narrow, or defer the order and seek further Washington review. He cannot command coalition units directly, guarantee Iraqi compliance, reconstruct vanished units by decree, or manufacture payroll records and barracks. His clock is days: his 19 May memorandum proposes issuing the order “in the coming days,” while coalition officers are already negotiating with former Iraqi generals and public order is poor.

## 4. Options genuinely on the table that day

1. **Sign the broad dissolution.** Bremer’s 19 May memorandum encloses the draft, dismisses employees of the Ministry of Defence and armed services, and proposes a new force built from individuals. He signed the final order on 23 May.
2. **Defer and continue selective recall.** The Army’s official history records that CFLCC was negotiating with senior Iraqi generals and had initial plans for a new ministry when it learned of the intended order; Jay Garner objected that ORHA had planned to bring the army back. This was active work, not a counterfactual invented later.
3. **Narrow the list: dissolve coercive regime organs, preserve or provisionally reconstitute regular forces after vetting.** The draft was already selective: Bremer retained the Interior Ministry because its police were needed to restore order. Garner’s objection and CFLCC’s recall-and-vetting work supplied the narrower institutional alternative.

## 5. Evidence horizon

**Known:** regular units had largely left their posts; facilities and records were damaged; coalition security capacity was strained; military planners were in contact with Iraqi officers; and Bremer’s memorandum estimated roughly 400,000 Ministry of Defence employees would be affected. The same memorandum expressly anticipated “serious discontent, increased terrorism, and much higher crime rates” if military and security-sector pensioners lost payments, while admitting rosters, contacts, costs and payment mechanics were not yet available.

**Reasonably inferable:** institutional dissolution would make coalition forces and a not-yet-built Iraqi force carry the security function; delayed severance would magnify the transition risk. **Uncertain:** how many personnel would return, whether Shia conscripts would serve under a predominantly Sunni officer corps, whether Kurds and Shia leaders would accept recall, and whether intact units could be vetted and controlled. **Unknowable that day:** the scale and composition of the later insurgency and the performance of the New Iraqi Army. Later protests, insurgent recruitment, training failures, the 7 August Order No. 22, and later stipend totals belong after the choice screen—not in it.

## 6. Who inherits it

Colonel **Paul Hughes**, CPA strategic adviser, had been meeting Iraqi generals to identify personnel for a future army; he had to restart with individuals. Lieutenant General **David McKiernan** and Major General **William Webster** of CFLCC still had to provide security while abandoning negotiations based on reconstituted units. Former Iraqi officers, conscripts and their families then had choices under lost status and uncertain pay: seek CPA screening and new employment, protest, withdraw, or join armed political and criminal groups. Their later choices must be reconstructed, not treated as an automatic reaction.

## 7. Live disputes to preserve

- **What existed to dissolve?** Bremer testified in 2007 that no formed army remained and reframed the choice as whether to recall it. Army accounts document ongoing negotiations, volunteers and plans to reuse former personnel. Both can be true in part; the chapter should not settle the institutional question by word choice.
- **Whose decision was it?** Bremer’s 19 May memorandum asks Rumsfeld to concur and copies Wolfowitz, Feith and Haynes; Bremer later said the policy was properly cleared. Garner, CENTCOM participants and other officials described surprise or inadequate interagency review. The signed legal act is Bremer’s, but Washington’s direction and assent remain disputed.
- **Causation:** official accounts disagree on whether dissolution materially fed insurgency or whether force dispersal, prior repression, looting, occupation policy and inadequate coalition strength dominate. Preserve this as a competing diagnosis.

## 9. Decision question

**May you abolish a failed institution before the replacement that must perform its essential function has an executable plan?** This is replacement latency and risk handoff after institutional collapse—not Bay of Pigs’ continuation after removal of a prerequisite, nor Triangle’s dependence on a person or key.

The chapter will explicitly decline to decide whether invading Iraq was legal, moral or strategically justified; none is necessary to play this narrower administrative choice.

## 10. Risks

This is post-invasion, so it abandons the dossier’s preferred phase-one authorization and can collapse into the familiar verdict that “disbanding caused the insurgency.” Ownership is vulnerable if further records show Bremer had a binding instruction rather than discretion; reversibility is vulnerable if the recall work had no recoverable units; causation is vulnerable if dissolution changed labels but not security capacity. **Kill the choice** if no contemporaneous record can establish that Bremer received the recall alternative before signature, if Washington’s instruction removed his authority to defer, or if the chapter cannot separate Order No. 2’s effect from the already completed self-demobilization and Order No. 1.
