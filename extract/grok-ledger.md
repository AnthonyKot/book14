- **p.2167 / L60634–60643 — Bostwick summation — CONTEMPORARY INTERPRETATION.** Motive for the locked door: “to search these poor employees’ bags as they went out at night”; cites Harris’s six arrests.

## 7. LEGAL FRAME

- **p.15 / L513–540 — Bostwick opening — CONTEMPORARY INTERPRETATION.** Theory: locked door = misdemeanor; death of Schwartz while so engaged = manslaughter; door “should be kept open during working hours, and unlocked.”
- **p.14 / L454–489 — Steuer / Crain on opening — CONTEMPORARY INTERPRETATION.** Steuer objects to DA stating the law; Crain: criticism “perhaps well founded”; Bostwick may outline theory only.
- **p.2055–2056 / L56710–56730 — Steuer summation, reading Section 80 — CONTEMPORARY INTERPRETATION (text).**  
  Handrails / rubber / screens (not this case), then: “All doors leading in or to any such factory shall be so constructed as to open outwardly where practicable, and shall not be locked, bolted or fastened during working hours.”
- **p.2190–2191 / L61247–61276 — Judge Crain, charge — CONTEMPORARY INTERPRETATION.** Same Section 80 sentence. “All” construed as **every** such door. Working hours include a reasonable time to exit after work stops. Penal Law §1275: violation of Labor Law art. 6 = misdemeanor.
- **p.2193–2194 / L61331–61360 — Judge Crain, charge (knowledge + causation) — CONTEMPORARY INTERPRETATION.** For *felony* manslaughter 1st, jury must find (1) the door locked, (2) locked under circumstances bringing **knowledge** to these defendants, (3) **cause and effect** between that lock and Schwartz’s death. Would she have lived if the door had not been locked and she had reached the WP stairs?
- **p.2195–2199 / L61372–61460 — Judge Crain, charge (manslaughter 2nd / culpable negligence) — CONTEMPORARY INTERPRETATION.** Culpable = criminal negligence, not civil. May consider a statutory breach as bearing on negligence if death was the direct result.
- **p.2203 / L61541–61557 — Steuer exceptions — CONTEMPORARY INTERPRETATION.** Excepts: (1) “all” = every door; (2) working hours include exit time; (3) definition of culpable negligence; (4) negligence from statutory breach / §80; (5) voluntary exclamations. “Those are the only exceptions.”
- **p.1870 / L55342–55348 — Crain, mid-trial — CONTEMPORARY INTERPRETATION.** “no provision of law that compels factory owners to have their employees go out of more than one door.”
- **p.2204 / L61563–61564 — Court minutes — OBSERVATION.** Jury out **about 2:55 p.m.**, back **4:45 p.m.**, verdict **not guilty.**
